## Why this matters

- **A motor spends years in stores, transport and launchers, and seconds firing.** The temperature around it cycles
  each day and each year.
- **Each cycle strains the bore and the bond,** and a viscoelastic propellant does not take each one back in full.
  Repeated cycles can crack a grain at a strain that it survives once.
- **The checks of the [grain page](../../motor/propellant-grain/) take one load at a time.** A service life needs the
  sum of all the loads, together with the ageing of the propellant.
- **The strength that the storage uses is not there for the cold ignition** at the end of it.

## The intuition: a paperclip and a rubber band

<Analogy title="A paperclip and a rubber band">
  Bend a paperclip once and it holds; bend it fifty times and it breaks. A rubber band left stretched around a box for
  a year cracks, although it would once have stretched further. A grain has both: cycles from the weather, and a strain
  that it holds for years. Neither one matters in a day; both add up.
</Analogy>

## The climate

- **The air around a store, a transport and a launcher swings** each day and each year. MIL-HDBK-310 gives the world's
  extremes; your environment specification gives those of your programme.
- **A shelter, a container or a heated store cuts the daily swing.** Sunshine on a case in the open adds to it.
- **Write the climate as a mean, a seasonal amplitude and a daily amplitude** for each phase of the life, with the time
  that the motor spends in each.

## How a cycle reaches the grain

- **A periodic temperature dies away into a solid.** In a thick solid its amplitude falls as $e^{-x/\delta}$ and lags by
  $x/\delta$ radians, with the penetration depth

$$
\delta = \sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{\omega}}
$$

  For a propellant with $\alpha = 2 \times 10^{-7}$ m²/s, $\delta$ is 75 mm for a daily cycle and 1.4 m for a seasonal
  one (Carslaw and Jaeger). A web much thicker than $\delta$ filters the cycle. A web of about $\delta$ does not: the
  bore is closed, so the whole web swings with the case.
- **The bore strain follows the mean temperature of the web.** The propellant is nearly incompressible, so the volume
  that it gains or loses goes to the bore. The bore strain follows the area-weighted mean temperature change of the
  propellant, $\Delta\bar T_p$, and that of the case, $\Delta T_c$:

$$
\varepsilon_\text{bore} = -\tfrac{3\alpha_p}{2}\,(m^2 - 1)\,\Delta\bar T_p + \tfrac{\alpha_c}{2}\,(3m^2 - 1)\,\Delta T_c
$$

  With one $\Delta T$ for both, this is the cool-down strain of the
  [grain page](../../motor/propellant-grain/#cool-down-why-the-bore-cracks).
- **A small grain feels the day; a large one feels only the season.** The area weighting puts most of the web near the
  case, where the daily cycle is strongest. The bore of the 300 mm grain feels 71% of a daily cycle; that of a 3 m
  grain only 12%, and 10 hours late. Both feel almost all of the seasonal cycle.
- **The bond feels the day in full.** The propellant next to the case follows the air, so the bond and the grain ends
  see the daily cycle even in a large motor.
- **A fast cycle still moves the case.** When the case warms before the grain, it stretches the grain: the case term
  does not lag.

## Damage

### Cycles: Miner's rule

- **The life at one strain.** Cyclic tests of the propellant, at the rate and the temperature of the cycles, give the
  number of cycles to failure at each strain. A power law often fits them:

$$
N_f = \left(\frac{\varepsilon_\text{cap}}{\varepsilon}\right)^{k}
$$

- **Miner's rule adds the fractions:** $D = \sum n_i / N_i$, and the grain fails at $D = 1$
  ([Fatigue](../../fundamentals/fatigue/#variable-loads-miners-rule)). For a climate, count each day with its peak
  strain: the mean strain, the seasonal strain of that day, and the daily amplitude.
- **The power law makes the cold months do the work.** With $k = 8$, a strain 9% higher doubles the damage of a day.
  In the worked example, the coldest quarter of the year does 60% of the damage and the colder half 89%. The climate
  matters far more than the count of the days.
- **Use the capability at the rate of the cycles.** A daily cycle is slow: its reduced rate is on the low branch of the
  master curve, often below the slowest tests
  ([Propellant mechanical tests](../../motor/propellant-tests/#from-the-tests-to-a-master-curve)).

### Time under the held strain

- **The mean strain is held for the life of the motor.** A propellant held at a strain or a stress fails in time, and
  sooner at a higher load. Tests at a constant strain or a constant load, at several levels and temperatures, give the
  time to failure.
- **The same rule adds this damage in time:** $D = \int dt / t_f$, with the time to failure $t_f$ from those tests,
  shifted to each temperature by $a_T$. Linear cumulative damage of this kind is a common basis of service-life
  prediction for grains (AGARD-CP-586). The calculator does not include it: add it from your own tests.

### The strength that is left

- **By Miner's rule, the ignition breaks the grain when its own damage fills the life that is left:**
  $(\varepsilon/\varepsilon_\text{cap})^k = 1 - D$. So a grain that has used a fraction $D$ of its life keeps a
  fraction $(1 - D)^{1/k}$ of its capability, if the same exponent holds at the rate of the ignition.
- **The steeper the law, the less strength the damage costs.** With $k = 8$, half the life costs 8% of the strength.
- **Multiply the capability at the cold ignition by it,** and check the ignition again at the end of the storage
  ([Propellant mechanical tests](../../motor/propellant-tests/#worked-example-an-allowable-for-the-300-mm-grain)).

## The service life

- **The service life is the shortest of several limits:** the time to the allowed damage; the time at which ageing and
  damage together take the margin of a load case to zero; the chemical life of the propellant, which your energetics
  specialists assess; and the life of the other parts ([Storage & ageing](../storage-ageing/)).
- **Programmes add the damage over the phases of the life:** the factory, the depot, the transport and the launcher.
  They often run it as a Monte Carlo over the climates and the properties, and they compare it with surveillance motors.
- **Milder storage buys life.** A heated store, a shade from the sun and a lower stress-free temperature all cut the
  strain, and with a steep law a small cut in the strain is a large cut in the damage.

## Worked example: the 300 mm grain in a cold depot

The grain is that of the [grain example](../../motor/propellant-grain/#worked-example-htpb-grain-in-the-d6ac-case): a
100 mm bore in a 300 mm OD, stress-free at 60 °C, with $\alpha_p = 100$ and $\alpha_c = 12.3$ µm/m·K. There is 2 mm of
EPDM between the grain and the case. The thermal properties of the propellant are illustrative: 0.45 W/m·K,
1770 kg/m³ and 1250 J/kg·K. The depot has a mean of −5 °C, ±15 K over the year and ±8 K over the day, for ten years,
with two cold soaks to −40 °C each year (10.4% at the bore each time). The damage values are **for this example
only**: a capability of 25% at the rate of the cycles (near the 24.7% that the propellant tests page gives for the slow
cool-down), an exponent of 8, and an allowed damage of 0.5. Push **Load worked example**.

1. **Into the grain.** The daily penetration depth is 75 mm and the web is 100 mm, so the daily cycle reaches all of the
   web, with 68% at the bore. The bore strain feels 71% of it, 2.7 hours after the air, and all of the seasonal cycle.
2. **The strains.** The cooling from 60 °C to the mean of −5 °C holds 6.76% at the bore. The seasons add ±1.56% and the
   days ±0.60%: 8.92% on a winter night.
3. **One year.** The 365 days use 0.0345 of the life, and the worst day $2.6 \times 10^{-4}$, in the middle of winter.
   The two cold soaks add 0.0018.
4. **Ten years:** $D = 10 \times (0.0345 + 0.0018) = 0.363$. At this rate the grain gets to the allowed 0.5 in
   13.8 years.
5. **The strength left** for the cold ignition is $(1 - 0.363)^{1/8} = 0.945$: the capability at the cold ignition is
   5.5% lower after the ten years.
6. **A temperate depot** (15 °C, ±10 K and ±6 K) peaks at 6.17%, 31% lower. In ten years its cycles use 0.0185 of the
   life: 19 times less. With the same cold soaks, the total is 0.036, and the soaks do half of it.
7. **A 3 m grain** in the same cold depot feels only 12% of the daily cycle at its bore. Its cycles use 0.0205 of the
   life each year, against 0.0345 for the 300 mm grain.

<Aside type="tip" title="What the example shows">
  The cycles of a cold depot use a third of this grain's life in ten years, most of it in the cold months. The strain,
  not the count of the days, decides: in a temperate depot the cycles of the same grain do 19 times less damage. And
  the damage costs the ignition less than it seems: a damage of 0.36 costs 5.5% of the strength.
</Aside>

## Where this stops

<Aside type="caution" title="A screen of the cycles">
  The calculator counts the damage of the thermal cycles at the bore. A service life also needs the time under the
  held strain, the bond and the grain ends, the ageing of the propellant, and the test data of your propellant.
</Aside>

- **The held strain.** The damage of the time under load is not in the calculator.
- **Viscoelastic stresses,** and the bond and the grain ends, which see the daily cycle in full.
- **Sunshine and wind.** A case in the sun can be much hotter than the air.
- **The order of the loads.** Miner's rule takes it as unimportant; for a propellant it can matter.
- **Ageing** changes the capability, and can change the stress-free temperature, over the life.
- **The sensitivity.** The result depends steeply on the exponent and on the capability at slow rates: both come from
  tests.

## On the drawing and in the specifications

- **The environments:** the temperature limits of storage, transport and the launcher, the climates, and the time in
  each.
- **The stress-free temperature** of the grain, and the cure that sets it.
- **The service life,** its basis, and the surveillance that confirms it.
- **The shelter and conditioning** that the life depends on.
