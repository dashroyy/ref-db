## Why this matters

- **The vehicle turns about its centre of gravity.** The steering moment is the side force times the arm from the
  nozzle pivot to the CG ([Thrust vector control](../thrust-vector-control/#side-force-and-what-it-costs)). The arm
  changes through the burn.
- **The inertia sets how fast it turns.** It falls by half or more through the burn. The control system needs the
  inertia at each instant, not one value.
- **An offset makes a disturbing moment.** A lateral offset of the CG and an offset of the thrust line make a moment
  that the steering must hold for the full burn
  ([Tolerance stack](../../drawing/tolerance-stack/#the-nozzle-alignment)).
- **Loads and handling use them.** The flight loads, the lift points, the cradles and the test stand all start from the
  mass and the CG ([Load paths](../../joints/load-paths/), [Handling & transport](../../verify/handling-and-transport/)).
- **Spinning stages must be balanced.** A CG or a principal axis off the spin axis makes the stage wobble.

## The intuition: a pole with a bucket

<Analogy title="A pole with a bucket of water">
  Carry a long pole with a full bucket of water hung at one end. The balance point is near the bucket, and the pole is
  hard to swing. Let the water run out: the balance point moves back toward the middle of the pole, and the pole swings
  easily. A motor does the same as it burns. The propellant is the water.
</Analogy>

## The mass properties table

Each part is one line: its mass, the position of its CG along the axis and across it, and its moments of inertia about
its own CG. Then

$$
m = \sum m_i
\qquad
\bar x = \frac{\sum m_i\,x_i}{m}
\qquad
\bar y = \frac{\sum m_i\,y_i}{m}
\qquad
I = \sum \left( I_i + m_i\,d_i^2 \right)
$$

where $d_i$ is the distance from the CG of each part to the axis through the CG of the whole (the parallel-axis
theorem). A radius of gyration $k$ is a short way to give an inertia: $I = m k^2$.

| Shape | Roll, about its own axis | Pitch, about a transverse axis through its CG |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thin cylindrical shell, radius $R$, length $L$ | $m R^2$ | $m\left(\frac{R^2}{2} + \frac{L^2}{12}\right)$ |
| Tube, bore radius $a$, outer radius $b$, length $L$ | $\frac{m}{2}\left(a^2 + b^2\right)$ | $m\left(\frac{a^2 + b^2}{4} + \frac{L^2}{12}\right)$ |
| Thin hemispherical dome, radius $R$, with its CG $R/2$ from its base | $\frac{2}{3} m R^2$ | $\frac{5}{12} m R^2$ |
| Solid cylinder: the tube with $a = 0$ | $\frac{1}{2} m R^2$ | $m\left(\frac{R^2}{4} + \frac{L^2}{12}\right)$ |

- **Fix the reference.** State the origin and the directions on the drawing: a datum of the case, with aft positive,
  or the axes of the vehicle from the ICD ([Requirements & ICD](../../fundamentals/requirements/)).
- **Give each line a status:** estimated, calculated from the model, or weighed. Carry a growth allowance on the lines
  that are not weighed yet. ANSI/AIAA S-120A sets out the allowances by status, and the mass properties control plan
  of a programme.
- **The burn changes three lines:** the propellant, the insulation that chars and erodes, and the nozzle that erodes.
  The propellant is by far the largest.

## Through the burn

### The centre of gravity moves

- **A tube with inhibited ends** burns from its bore. Its own CG stays at its middle, but its mass falls, so the total
  CG moves toward the CG of the inert parts. With a heavy nozzle, the CG of the motor moves aft.
- **In the vehicle, the propellant is aft of the CG** of the whole, because the payload is forward. As the propellant
  burns, the CG of the vehicle moves forward: the other way from the motor's.
- **An end burner** burns from its aft face, so its own CG moves forward through the whole burn. It has the largest
  travel of any grain.
- **A grain that burns faster at one end** (fins at one end, a tapered port,
  [erosive burning](../erosive-burning/#the-aft-end-burns-through-first)) moves its CG away from that end.

### The inertia falls

The propellant is most of the mass, and it is near the CG of the motor. At burnout, the inertia is that of the case,
the nozzle, the closures and the payload. In the 300 mm motor below, the pitch inertia of the vehicle falls by half,
and that of the motor alone by two thirds. The angular acceleration for a nozzle angle $\delta$,

$$
\ddot\theta = \frac{F \sin\delta \; L}{I}
$$

rises by more than the inertia falls, because the arm $L$ also grows.

### A bore off the axis

Let the axis of the bore be a distance $e$ from the axis of the case. The propellant is the disc of the case less the
hole, so its first moment about the axis is that of the hole, with the other sign:

$$
m_p\,\bar y_p = -\,\rho_p\,L\,\pi a^2\,e
$$

- **It grows as the bore burns open,** with $a^2$, while the mass of the motor falls. The lateral offset of the CG of
  the motor grows from a very small value at ignition to its largest when the web first burns through, on the thin
  side.
- **Then a sliver is left** on the thick side: a crescent with its CG about half a radius off the axis. As the sliver
  burns, the offset goes away.
- **At web burnout,** $a = R - e$, and the offset of the motor is

$$
\bar y = \frac{\rho_p\,L\,\pi\,(R - e)^2\,e}{m_\text{burnout}}
$$

- **So the position tolerance of the bore sets the lateral offset at burnout,** not at ignition. Other causes act in
  the same way: a case wall that is thicker on one side, insulation of uneven thickness, a propellant density that is
  not uniform, and a burn rate that is higher on one side, for example in a motor that the sun heats on one side before
  launch.

## Steering, misalignment and trim

The steering must hold the moment of the thrust about the CG:

$$
M = F\,(d + \bar y)
\qquad\qquad
\delta_\text{trim} = \arcsin\frac{d + \bar y}{L}
$$

Here $d$ is the offset of the thrust line at the CG ([Tolerance stack](../../drawing/tolerance-stack/#the-nozzle-alignment)),
$\bar y$ the lateral offset of the CG, and $L$ the arm from the pivot to the CG. All three change through the burn.
Check the trim at ignition, at the largest offset and at burnout.

- **A vehicle without thrust vector control** has no trim. The misalignment turns the vehicle, and the guidance, the
  fins or a spin must correct it.
- **Spinning stages** spin to average the misalignment out. They must be balanced: a CG off the spin axis (static
  unbalance) and a principal axis tilted from the spin axis (dynamic unbalance) make the stage cone. A spin balance
  machine measures both, and small masses correct them.
- **The spin axis must be stable.** A rigid body spins steadily about its axis of largest or of smallest inertia. If the
  body loses energy, through liquids or flexible parts, only the axis of largest inertia is stable (Thomson). The burn
  changes the inertias, so check their ratio through the burn.

## Measuring mass properties

- **Mass.** Weigh on calibrated load cells. Weigh the inert motor before the cast and the loaded motor after it: the
  difference is the propellant, and it checks the casting.
- **Axial CG.** Weigh on two supports (or three), with the readings net of the fixture:

$$
\bar x = \frac{W_1\,x_1 + W_2\,x_2}{W_1 + W_2}
$$

- **Lateral CG: the reversal.** Read the offset, turn the motor 180° about its axis on the fixture, and read again. The
  error of the fixture is the same in both readings: half the difference is the motor, and half the sum is the fixture.
- **Moments of inertia: pendulums.** On a filar pendulum, the motor hangs on two or three parallel wires of length $h$,
  each a distance $r$ from the vertical axis through its CG, and swings about that axis with a period $T$:

$$
I = \frac{m\,g\,r^2\,T^2}{4\pi^2\,h}
$$

  Swing the fixture alone and subtract it. A compound pendulum, swung about a pivot a distance $d$ from the CG, gives
  $I = m g d\,T^2/(4\pi^2) - m d^2$. These are the methods of NACA Report 467, for aircraft.
- **Uncertainty.** $T$ and $r$ enter squared. Time many cycles, measure $r$ between the attachment points of the wires,
  and keep the fixture light: its inertia is subtracted, but its uncertainty stays.
- **Spin balance machines** measure the lateral CG and the products of inertia of spinning stages.
- **A loaded motor is an explosive article.** Measure it under the explosive-safety rules of the facility, with fixtures
  and lifts that the facility has approved ([Handling & transport](../../verify/handling-and-transport/)).

## Worked example: the 300 mm motor and its payload

The masses are those of the [hardware page](../hardware/#worked-example-the-300-mm-motor-from-the-other-pages): the
case 11.77 kg, the insulation 4.84 kg, the propellant 111.2 kg, the nozzle 6 kg and other hardware 4 kg. The domes are
taken 75 mm deep, so the case is 1150 mm long over its domes, as on the
[handling page](../../verify/handling-and-transport/). x is measured aft from the apex of the forward dome. The
positions of the nozzle and the other hardware are illustrative. The 50 kg payload is at x = −120 mm, and the nozzle
pivot is at the aft joint, x = 1110 mm. The limits are **for this example only**: a lateral offset of 1.0 mm for the CG
of the vehicle, and an uncertainty of 1% (k = 2) on the moments of inertia. Push **Load worked example**.

1. **The table at ignition.** The case and insulation are 16.61 kg at 575 mm, the propellant 111.21 kg at 575 mm, the
   nozzle 6 kg at 1170 mm, and the forward and aft hardware 2 kg each at 60 and 1110 mm. The motor is 137.82 kg with
   its CG at 601.2 mm. With the payload, the vehicle is 187.82 kg at 409.2 mm: 700.8 mm forward of the pivot. This is
   the 700 mm that the [TVC example](../thrust-vector-control/#worked-example-a-flexible-bearing-for-the-300-mm-motor)
   and the [tolerance stack](../../drawing/tolerance-stack/#the-nozzle-alignment) assumed.
2. **At burnout,** the motor is 26.61 kg at 710.7 mm: its CG moves 109 mm aft, toward the heavy nozzle. The vehicle is
   76.61 kg at 168.5 mm: its CG moves 241 mm forward. The arm grows from 701 to 941 mm.
3. **Inertia.** The pitch inertia of the vehicle falls from 35.6 to 18.2 kg·m², and that of the motor alone from 15.6 to
   5.24 kg·m². With 29.5 kN of thrust, 1° of nozzle angle turns the vehicle at 10.1 rad/s² at ignition and at
   26.7 rad/s² at burnout: 2.6 times more. The control gains must follow the burn.
4. **A bore 0.5 mm off the axis** (illustrative). At ignition, the CG of the motor is 0.050 mm off the axis. The web
   first burns through when 99.25% of the propellant has burned. A sliver of 0.83 kg is then left, with its CG 74.6 mm
   off the axis, and the CG of the motor is 2.26 mm off the axis: 45 times its value at ignition. The CG of the vehicle
   goes from 0.037 to 0.80 mm: 80% of the 1.0 mm limit, so it passes.
5. **Trim.** With the 0.45 mm thrust line offset of the tolerance stack, the trim is 0.040° at ignition and 0.077° at
   web burnout, and the disturbing moment goes from 14.4 to 36.9 N·m. The TVC example allowed 1 mm of CG offset and
   0.12° of trim, so this bore tolerance fits inside it.
6. **Weighing.** On supports at 200 and 1000 mm, with a fixture tare of 10 kg at each, the loaded motor reads 78.71 and
   79.12 kg: 137.83 kg with its CG at 601.2 mm. The reversal reads 0.42 mm, then 0.32 mm: the motor is 0.05 mm off the
   axis, and the fixture 0.37 mm.
7. **The pendulum.** On wires 400 mm from the axis and 2500 mm long, with a 20 kg fixture, the motor swings in 2.587 s
   and the fixture alone in 1.943 s. That gives 15.59 ± 0.10 kg·m² (k = 2, ±0.63%), inside the 1%. The radius of the
   wires gives 64% of the variance and the period 28%: here, 0.5 mm on r costs more than 2 ms on T.

<Aside type="tip" title="What the example shows">
  The motor and the vehicle move their CGs in opposite directions, and the steering needs the vehicle's. The inertia
  halves while the arm grows, so the response to the nozzle more than doubles. And a 0.5 mm bore offset that is
  invisible at ignition gives the largest lateral offset of the flight, at web burnout.
</Aside>

## Where this stops

<Aside type="caution" title="A rigid, one-axis table">
  These are the mass properties of rigid parts on one axis, with a grain that burns at a uniform rate. They are the
  start of a mass properties analysis, not the record of a real motor, which comes from its model and its measurements.
</Aside>

- **Products of inertia,** offsets in two planes, and the dynamic balance of spinning stages.
- **Real burn-back:** slivers of star and slotted grains, erosive burning, and a burn rate that is not uniform.
- **The insulation and the nozzle** lose mass as they char and erode, and the gas in the case has mass too.
- **Jet damping:** the flow through the nozzle carries away angular momentum and damps the turning a little (Thomson).
- **Grain slump and deformation** under acceleration move the CG a little.
- **Pendulum corrections:** large angles, the stiffness of the wires, and the air, which adds mass and damping to the
  swing of a large part.

## On the drawing and in the specifications

- **The mass and the CG,** with their tolerances, and the reference for x and y.
- **The position of the bore** relative to the axis of the case, with the tolerance that the lateral CG budget allows.
- **The measurement:** which motors (each one or a sample), which properties, the method, and the uncertainty.
- **The CG marked on the motor** for handling.
- **For spinning stages:** the balance requirement and the balance masses.
