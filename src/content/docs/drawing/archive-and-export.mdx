## Why this matters

A motor can stay in service for many years after its drawings are released. During that time somebody will need the
record: for a failure investigation, a spare part, a life extension or an audit. The CAD software that made the files
will have changed many times. A record that only that software can open is not a record.

Technical data also leaves your organisation: to suppliers, to partners, to colleagues overseas. Each transfer can be
an export, and the sender is responsible for it. Rocket motor hardware and its technical data are often on the
control lists.

<Analogy title="A letter in a sealed envelope">
  A record is like a letter that must be read in 30 years. Write it in a language that will still be read (a neutral
  format), seal the envelope (a checksum that shows nobody changed it), and write on the envelope who may open it
  (the export-control marking).
</Analogy>

## Archive formats

| Content | Format | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawings | PDF/A (ISO 19005) | Self-contained: fonts and images are embedded. Made for long-term reading |
| 3D models with PMI | STEP AP242 (ISO 10303-242) | Neutral. Carries the geometry with its tolerances and annotation |
| Native CAD | Keep, but not as the record | Becomes unreadable as software versions move on |
| Reports, inspection and FAI records | PDF/A, with data as CSV | Plain-text data stays readable without the software that made it |

- **PDF/A has parts.** PDF/A-2 suits most drawings. PDF/A-3 can also embed other files, for example the STEP file
  inside the drawing PDF.
- **For model-based definition, the PMI must be semantic,** not only a picture of the annotation. Otherwise the model
  that is the master cannot be read by the inspection software. The aerospace practice for long-term archiving of 3D
  data is EN 9300 (LOTAR).
- **Release the native files with the record, as reference.** They make the next change easier, but they do not
  define the part.

## How long, and how to show integrity

- **Keep the record for the period your contract requires.** This is often the service life of the product plus a
  defined number of years. Use the contract values: the tool does not supply them.
- **Store a checksum of each file.** A SHA-256 checksum changes completely if one bit of the file changes. A manifest
  of checksums, kept with the package, shows later that each file is the one that was released.
- **Check the files during the retention period.** Media fail and formats age. Move the files to new media, and check
  the checksums each time.

## Export control

- **Classify the technical data** against the UK Strategic Export Control Lists (military and dual-use) before it
  leaves your organisation. Your export compliance team does the classification.
- **Mark the drawings and the file metadata** with the classification.
- **Any transfer can be an export.** Email, a cloud share, a shared screen and a drive in your luggage are all ways
  to transfer technology.
- **US-origin content can bring ITAR or EAR obligations** on top of UK controls. They follow the data: a re-transfer
  can need US authorisation. US rules also treat a release to a foreign person inside the US as an export.
- **An export needs the correct licence:** a SIEL, an OIEL or an applicable OGEL. Your export compliance team decides;
  the designer marks.

<Aside type="caution" title="Trap">
  Sending a drawing to a supplier or an overseas colleague without checking its classification. The sender is
  responsible, not the recipient.
</Aside>

## Calculator

**Package** lists the items in the release with the format of each one, and gives the year until which the
record must be kept. The service life and the years after service start blank: they come from your contract.
**Checksums** computes the SHA-256 of each file you choose. The files stay on your device: your browser computes the
hashes. It gives a manifest to copy, and checks files against an old manifest. **Send check** lists the decisions
that must be recorded before technical data is sent, and gives the marking to fill. It never gives a classification
or a licence.

## Worked example: archiving and sending the aft closure

The aft closure is released at revision J ([Revision & sign-off](../revision-and-sign-off/#worked-example-a-deeper-gland-in-the-aft-closure)).
Select **Load worked example**.

1. **The package.** The drawing as PDF/A, the model as STEP AP242 with its PMI, the stress report, the inspection and
   FAI records, and the material certificates are the record (5 items). The native CAD is kept for reference only.
2. **How long.** For this example only, take a 15-year service life and 10 years after service. A release in 2026
   gives a record kept until **2051**: 25 years. The files must stay readable for all that time, so plan to check them
   and move them to new media.
3. **Checksums.** Choose the release files on the Checksums tab and copy the manifest into the package. To check the
   tool, hash a text file that holds only `abc` with no line end. Its SHA-256 is
   `ba7816bf8f01cfea414140de5dae2223b00361a396177a9cb410ff61f20015ad` (the FIPS 180-2 test vector).
4. **Send check.** A machine shop outside the UK will quote for the closure. The data is not classified yet, and
   no licence decision is recorded. The check gives **Stop: do not send yet**, with 2 decisions missing. Ask the export
   compliance team. When they have classified the data and recorded the licence route, mark the drawing and the file
   metadata, and then send.

## Where this stops

- **No classification and no licence.** The tool lists the decisions; your export compliance team makes them.
- **Not legal advice.** Export law changes, and the control lists change. Use your company's export procedures.
- **Retention periods and formats come from your contract** and your company's records procedure.
- **Checksums show integrity, not authenticity.** A digital signature shows who released a file. A checksum shows
  only that the file has not changed since the manifest was made.

## On the drawing

- **The export-control marking** in the title block and in the file metadata.
- **The master stated:** drawing or model ([Revision & sign-off](../revision-and-sign-off/#the-master-drawing-or-model)).
- **The release in neutral formats,** with a checksum manifest kept with it.
