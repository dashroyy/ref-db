## Why this matters

A drawing is short because it borrows. "To BS 8888" brings in the meaning of every symbol; "AMS 6431" brings in the
chemistry, the melting practice and the tests of the steel; "ASTM E1444" brings in how the part is inspected. Each
reference is a part of the requirement that the drawing does not write out.

That only works if the reference is exact. A standard that was withdrawn twenty years ago, an undated reference that
changed under the supplier, or two tolerancing systems on one sheet all give a part that passes inspection and still
does not do what you meant.

<Analogy title="A recipe that says 'as Grandma made it'">
  A recipe that says "make the sauce as Grandma did" is short, and it works only if everyone has the same Grandma and
  she never changed her mind. A reference to a standard is the same: it saves writing, but only if it names one
  document, at one issue, that the reader can get.
</Analogy>

## Order of precedence

When documents disagree, the one higher in the list governs. The contract usually says so explicitly: check it.

1. **The contract.**
2. **The programme specification** and the interface control documents.
3. **Company procedures and standards.**
4. **National and international standards** that the documents above invoke.

A standard has no force on its own. It applies because a contract, a specification or a drawing invokes it, and only
to the extent that they say.

## Cite the issue

- **Write the year or the revision:** BS 8888:2020, AS9102C, ASTM E1444/E1444M-22, MMPDS with the edition your stress
  office uses. An undated reference follows whatever was published last, so the requirement can change after the drawing
  is released.
- **Name the clauses or the class** where the standard offers a choice: ISO 2768-mK, not ISO 2768; the acceptance
  class of an NDT method; the type and class of an anodic coating.
- **Use one tolerancing system.** ISO GPS (BS 8888, ISO 8015) and ASME Y14.5 look the same on the page and are read
  differently: the same frame means different things under ISO independency and ASME Rule #1
  ([Drawing frame & notes](../drawing-frame/#iso-and-asme-read-size-differently)). The title block says which system
  governs.

## Where to get them

| Publisher | Standards | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BSI, ISO | BS, BS EN, BS ISO, ISO | BSI Knowledge, iso.org. Many employers have a subscription: ask before you buy |
| ASME | Y14.5, Y14.35, B1.1, BPVC | asme.org |
| SAE International | AMS, AS, ARP, EIA-649, CMH-17 | sae.org |
| UK Defence Standardization | DEF STAN | Free to register; some parts are restricted to the Design Authority |
| NATO Standardization Office | STANAG, AOP | Public documents free; others through your national authority |
| NASA | NASA-STD | standards.nasa.gov: free |
| US DoD | MIL-STD, MIL-HDBK | ASSIST Quick Search: free |
| ASTM International | ASTM | astm.org |
| ECSS | ECSS-E-ST | ecss.nl: free after registration |

**Never design from a copy of unknown issue.** A PDF found on the web may be the wrong edition, or a draft. Get the
document from its publisher or your company library, and record the issue you used.

## Tailoring and deviations

Tailoring is legitimate; silent tailoring is not. If a part does not meet a clause of an invoked standard, record the
deviation with its justification and get it agreed by the authority that invoked the standard. The register of
deviations is part of the release ([Revision & sign-off](../revision-and-sign-off/)).

## Worked example: the references on the aft closure drawing

A first draft of the aft closure drawing lists these references: BS 8888, ISO 2768, ASME Y14.5, AMS 6431,
MIL-HDBK-5, ISO 4287 Ra 1.6, BS EN 10204 3.1, ASTM E1444 and AS9102. They are the default text of **Check
references**.

1. **The tolerancing system is mixed.** BS 8888 invokes ISO GPS, and ASME Y14.5 is the US system. Keep BS 8888, take
   out ASME Y14.5, and state the ISO system in the title block.
2. **Two references are withdrawn.** MIL-HDBK-5 was replaced by MMPDS in 2003, and ISO 4287 by ISO 21920-2 in 2021.
3. **Seven references are undated,** and ISO 2768 has no class.
4. **The corrected list** passes the check: BS 8888:2020, ISO 2768-mK:1989, AMS 6431 with its revision letter, MMPDS
   with the edition your stress office uses, ISO 21920-2:2021 for Ra 1.6, BS EN 10204:2004 type 3.1,
   ASTM E1444/E1444M-22 and AS9102C.

The issues in this example show the form of a dated reference. Use the issues that your contract invokes.

## Where this stops

- **The checker reads patterns.** It knows the register and a list of withdrawn standards. It cannot tell if an issue
  is the current one, or if the contract invokes a different one.
- **The register is a starting list,** not a complete one. Your programme's document list governs.
- **Out of scope:** standards for energetic materials, their qualification and stability, and initiation systems sit
  with your energetics and safety specialists.

## On the drawing

- **The governing standard and its issue** in the title block: BS 8888:2020, or ASME Y14.5-2018.
- **Every reference dated,** with the class or type where the standard offers a choice.
- **One tolerancing system** per drawing.
- **Deviations** from invoked standards recorded and agreed, not left silent.
