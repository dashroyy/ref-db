## Why this matters

Most rework does not come from the analysis. It comes from a detail: an O-ring groove with a sharp edge, a tapped
hole too shallow for its drill, an anodised bore that no longer takes its pin, a bracket that cracks on the press
brake. Each of these has a known rule. These guides put the rules in one place, and each ends with a callout in the
form it takes on the drawing. Replace the values in square brackets with your own.

Where a zone of this site covers a subject in full, the guide is a summary and links to it. Sheet metal, edges,
castings and forgings, bonding and springs have no zone of their own, so their guides go further, and the last three
have a tool.

<Analogy title="A pre-flight checklist">
  A pilot knows how to fly. The checklist is for the items that are easy to forget when everything else goes well. A
  design guide is the same: not a course in the subject, but the short list that stops a known mistake from reaching
  the shop.
</Analogy>

## Joints and hardware

### O-rings and static seals

- **Choose the standard first.** BS ISO 3601 for new metric designs, AS568 for US-origin equipment, BS 4518 and
  BS 1806 on legacy UK hardware. Do not put a ring of one standard in a groove of another.
- **Squeeze:** 15 to 30% for a static radial seal, 20 to 30% for a face seal, 10 to 20% reciprocating, 5 to 15%
  rotary. **Groove fill** 60 to 85%, never above about 90%: rubber does not compress, and it swells.
- **Stretch** on the inside diameter 1 to 5%. Above about 10 MPa, or with a large extrusion gap, add back-up rings.
- **Dimension the groove, not the ring.** A 15 to 20° lead-in, and no sharp edge anywhere the ring slides. Counterface
  Ra 1.6 for a static seal, 0.4 to 0.8 for a dynamic one.
- **Compound with its hardness:** NBR for mineral oil and fuel; FKM for fuel and hot oil, not phosphate esters; EPDM
  for phosphate esters, water and brake fluid, never mineral oil.

The groove at its tolerance corners, the compounds and the test: [O-ring design](../../seals/o-ring-design/) and
[Sealing strategy](../../seals/sealing-strategy/).

```text wrap
ITEM 7: O-RING, BS ISO 3601-1, [ID × CS], FKM [75] IRHD, GRADE N
GROOVE: DIMENSIONS AND TOLERANCES AS SHOWN, Ra 0.8 ON SEALING FACES
LEAD-IN 15° MIN. NO SHARP EDGES ON THE SEAL PATH. FIT SEAL LIGHTLY GREASED PER [ASSY SPEC].
```

### Bolts and bolted joints

- **Property class:** 8.8 means Rm 800 MPa and a yield of 0.8 × 800 = 640 MPa; 10.9 is 1000 and 900; 12.9 is 1200 and
  1080. Stainless A2-70 and A4-70: Rm 700 MPa, 0.2% proof about 450 MPa.
- **Call standard parts by standard, size, class and finish:** ISO 4014 and 4017 hexagon bolts and screws, ISO 4762
  socket head, ISO 4032 nuts, ISO 7089 washers. On aerospace hardware, call the part number (NAS, MS, AN or the
  programme's) and allow no substitutes: plating changes both strength and friction.
- **Engagement in a tapped hole:** at least 1d in steel, 1.5d in aluminium, 2d in plastics, or use an insert.
- **Torque is a stand-in for preload.** T ≈ K·F·d is a first estimate; K changes with lubrication and plating, so
  state every torque with its lubrication condition. Preload and torque from VDI 2230:
  [Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/).
- **Lock on purpose:** an all-metal prevailing-torque nut where it is hot, a nylon insert below about 120 °C, an
  anaerobic adhesive, or a positive lock. A plain spring washer does not lock under vibration:
  [Locking & retention](../../joints/locking-and-retention/).
- **Edges and pitch:** hole centre at least 2d from an edge in sheet, and about 4d between fasteners.

```text wrap
BOM: BOLT, HEX HD, ISO 4014 - M8 × 45 - 10.9, [FINISH SPEC]
WASHER ISO 7089 - 8 - 300 HV UNDER HEAD AND NUT
NOTE: TIGHTEN M8 BOLTS TO [24] N·m ±10%, THREADS LUBRICATED WITH [SPEC]
```

### Threads and tapped holes

- **Metric callout:** M8 × 1.25 - 6H, that is size, pitch and tolerance class. 6H for holes, 6g for external threads.
  Write the pitch even when it is coarse.
- **Blind holes:** give the full-thread depth the joint needs. The shop adds the tap run-out (2 to 3 pitches) and the
  drill point. Where space is tight, give the thread depth and the maximum drill depth. Countersink the entry 90°.
- **Inserts** (wire thread or key-locked) in aluminium, magnesium and plastics, and wherever a joint is often
  reassembled. Tap the hole to the insert maker's data.
- **Flight-critical external threads** are often MJ or UNJ, with a controlled root radius, and rolled after heat
  treatment. Do not draw a cut thread where the stress report assumed a rolled MJ thread.

Limits of size, thread classes and the stress area: [Threads](../../joints/threads/).

```text wrap
4 × M6 × 1 - 6H ↧ 12 MIN FULL THREAD, DRILL Ø5 ↧ 16 MAX
M10 × 1.5 - 6H THRU, CSK 90° × Ø10.5 BOTH SIDES
INSERT: [WIRE THREAD INSERT] M8 × 1.25 × 1.5D. TAP TO THE INSERT MAKER'S DATA.
```

### Limits, fits and dowels

- **Hole basis:** keep the hole at H (it is drilled and reamed) and change the shaft. H7/f7 running, H7/g6 close
  sliding, H7/h6 location, H7/k6 and H7/n6 transition, H7/p6 and H7/s6 press and shrink.
- **Rolling bearings:** the ring that turns relative to the load takes the interference. Then use the bearing maker's
  tables.
- **Dowels:** two pins locate a plate. Make the first hole round and the second a slot in line with it (or use a
  diamond pin), so the centre-distance tolerance does not lock the plate. Press each pin into one part and let it slip
  in the other.
- **Blind dowel holes need a vent:** trapped air stops the pin seating, and stops it ever coming out.

Hole and shaft limits from ISO 286 and the fit at temperature: [Fits & limits](../fits-and-limits/).

```text wrap
Ø10 H7 REAM ↧ 20, VENT Ø2 THRU. DOWEL ISO 8734 - 10 × 24 - A - St
SECOND POSITION: SLOT 10 H7 × 12, IN LINE WITH THE FIRST
```

## Surfaces and edges

### Surface texture

- **Ra by process:** sand cast 12.5 to 25; milled or turned 3.2, or 1.6 with a finishing pass; ground 0.4 to 0.8;
  honed 0.1 to 0.4; lapped below 0.1.
- **The cost step is below Ra 0.8:** it usually needs a second process, with its own set-up and inspection. Ask for it
  only where a seal slides, a bearing runs or fatigue needs it.
- **One general note,** then a local symbol only where the surface must be finer. ISO 21920 replaced ISO 1302 and
  ISO 4287 in 2021; Ra means the same.

Symbols, lay and the parameters beyond Ra: [Surface finish](../surface-finish/).

```text wrap
GENERAL: √ Ra 3.2 UNLESS OTHERWISE STATED (ISO 21920-1)
LOCAL: √ Ra 0.4 GROUND, LAY ⊥ TO AXIS (SEAL JOURNAL)
```

### Surface treatments and plating

- **Sulphuric anodise** (5 to 25 µm) for corrosion and paint key. It insulates: mask the earth points.
- **Hard anodise** (25 to 75 µm) for wear. About half of it grows outward: say whether dimensions apply before or
  after.
- **Conversion coating** has almost no thickness and conducts: use it for bond, earth and EMC faces. Trivalent
  coatings replace hexavalent chromate under REACH.
- **On steel,** zinc-nickel replaces cadmium on new parts. **Stainless steel** is passivated after machining.
- **Electroplated high-strength steel** (about 1000 MPa and above) is baked soon after plating to drive out hydrogen.
  Put the bake on the drawing or in the process specification.
- **Every finish note** names the governing specification, the masking, and when the dimensions apply.

Thickness growth, selection and the galvanic pairs: [Coatings](../../materials/coatings/).

```text wrap
FINISH: SULPHURIC ACID ANODISE TO [DEF STAN 03-SERIES PART / COMPANY SPEC], SEALED
MASK ALL TAPPED HOLES AND FACE A (BOND AREA: CONVERSION COATING ONLY)
DIMENSIONS APPLY AFTER TREATMENT
```

### Edges and corners

- **ISO 13715 makes an edge measurable.** Replace "break all sharp edges" with the edge symbol or a general note. A
  minus value is an undercut, material removed; a plus value is a burr, material allowed to stay. "−0.1/−0.5" means
  each external edge is removed by 0.1 to 0.5 mm, and no burr is allowed.
- **Chamfer where people and seals touch:** a defined chamfer (C0.5) where it matters, the general edge note elsewhere.
- **Internal corners of pockets:** the radius must be at least the cutter's. Use one value for all of them, slightly
  larger than a standard cutter (R5.2 rather than R5, so the cutter does not dwell in the corner), and larger in deep
  pockets: about a third of the depth keeps the cutter stiff.
- **Fillets at changes of section** on any part that sees fatigue. A sharp internal corner is where the crack starts.
- **Before release:** the title block gives the governing standard (BS 8888), the projection, the general tolerance,
  the general surface texture, the edge note, material with condition, finish, and part marking. Every feature is
  dimensioned once, from the datum that matters, and inspection can accept or reject every line on the sheet.

```text wrap
EDGES ISO 13715: −0.1/−0.5 UNLESS OTHERWISE STATED. NO BURRS.
POCKET CORNER RADII R5.2 UNLESS OTHERWISE STATED.
MARK PART NO. AND ISSUE PER [COMPANY SPEC], LOCATION SHOWN ⌂
```

## Making it

### Welded fabrications

- **ISO 2553 symbols:** the arrow points at the joint; the weld symbol on the solid reference line is the arrow side,
  on the dashed line the other side. ASME reverses this logic, so state the system.
- **Fillet size:** a4 is a 4 mm throat, z4 a 4 mm leg. A circle at the bend means all round; a flag means a site weld.
- **Control what costs:** the quality level (ISO 5817 B, C or D) and the NDT extent. Machine precise faces after
  welding. 6082 and 5083 weld (6082 loses strength in the heat-affected zone); 2024 and 7075 do not arc-weld.
- **Keep welds out of the highest stress:** a weld is the fatigue detail of the part.

Weld processes, procedures and their defects: [Welding](../../make/welding/).

```text wrap
WELD: FILLET a4 ALL ROUND, SYMBOLS TO ISO 2553. QUALITY ISO 5817 LEVEL C.
WELD PROCEDURE QUALIFIED TO ISO 15614-[PART]. MACHINE FACE B AFTER WELDING.
```

### Sheet metal and bending

When sheet bends, the outside stretches and the inside compresses. Between them is the neutral axis, which keeps its
length: the flat pattern is measured along it.

- **K-factor** is the depth of the neutral axis as a fraction of the thickness: about 0.33 to 0.42 for soft material
  air-bent, 0.45 to 0.5 for hard or bottomed material. It depends on the tooling, so use the value of the shop that
  bends the part.
- **Bend allowance** BA = θ·(R + K·t), the neutral axis through the bend. **Bend deduction**
  BD = 2·tan(θ/2)·(R + t) − BA. The flat length is the sum of the outside dimensions (to the virtual sharps) less one
  BD for each bend.
- **Smallest inside radius:** about 1t for soft aluminium and mild steel; 2t to 4t for 6082-T6, 6061-T6, 2024-T3
  and 7075-T6, which crack if bent tighter. Bend across the rolling direction where you can.
- **Shortest flange** about 4t + R, or it does not clear the die. **Holes** at least 2.5t + R from the bend, or they
  distort. **Bend relief** at least t wide where a bend meets an edge, or it tears.
- **One radius for every bend:** each radius is another tool and another set-up.
- **On the drawing:** dimension to the outside faces throughout and say so; give the inside radius and the thickness;
  give the flat pattern for reference, or the K-factor or bend deduction you used. Never dimension from a tangent
  point: nobody can find it.

The **Sheet metal** tool below draws the part and its flat pattern.

```text wrap
MATERIAL: 1.5 THK EN AW-5251-H22 SHEET TO BS EN 485-2
BEND: R2.0 INSIDE, K-FACTOR [0.38], ALL BENDS 90° ±1° UNLESS STATED
DIMENSIONS TO OUTSIDE FACES. FLAT PATTERN FOR REFERENCE ONLY. GRAIN DIRECTION AS SHOWN.
```

### Castings and forgings

- **Draft:** 1 to 3° for sand castings, 0.5 to 2° for investment castings, more in deep pockets. Show the draft
  direction and the parting line, or the foundry chooses them for you.
- **Even sections:** a sudden change of thickness causes shrinkage porosity and hot tears. Blend with generous radii,
  and taper the sections towards the feeder.
- **Machining allowance:** typically 2 to 4 mm on sand castings, 0.5 to 1.5 mm on investment castings. Mark which
  faces are machined.
- **Datum targets** locate an as-cast surface: a rough face makes a poor datum feature on its own
  ([Datums & position](../datums-and-position/)).
- **Acceptance:** give the radiographic level (BS EN 12681-1) and the surface condition (BS EN 1370) for each zone.
- **Forgings give grain flow,** and the grain flow is why the part is strong. Align it with the load path and enforce
  it with a macro-etch on the first article. Properties are directional: longitudinal is best, short-transverse the
  weakest.
- **The short-transverse direction** is where stress-corrosion and sustained-load failures of high-strength
  aluminium and steel start. If the part loads a forging or a thick plate through its thickness, decide it early: it
  changes the material and the temper, not only the analysis.

```text wrap
CASTING: [ALLOY] TO [SPEC]. DRAFT 2° UNLESS STATED, PARTING LINE AS SHOWN.
MACHINING ALLOWANCE 3 ON FACES MARKED ▽. AS-CAST SURFACES TO BS EN 1370 [CLASS].
RADIOGRAPHY TO BS EN 12681-1, SEVERITY LEVEL [x] IN ZONES SHOWN.
FORGING: GRAIN FLOW TO FOLLOW THE CONTOUR SHOWN. VERIFY BY MACRO-ETCH ON FIRST ARTICLE.
```

### Metal additive manufacturing

- **Build orientation is a material property:** properties in the build direction are lower, most of all in fatigue.
  Put the orientation on the drawing when it matters.
- **Overhangs below about 45° need support,** and supports in an internal passage may be impossible to remove. Draw
  teardrop or diamond holes instead of round ones.
- **Stress relieve before the part leaves the build plate,** and HIP fatigue- and fracture-critical parts. Design to
  the properties after HIP.
- **Every internal cavity needs an exit for the powder,** and a way to prove that it is clean.
- **As-built surfaces are about Ra 6 to 20:** call out every surface that must be machined, with stock on it.

Process selection, AM qualification and the cost: [Process & AM](../../make/process-and-am/).

### Choosing an NDT method

- **Visual** first, always. **Penetrant** (BS EN ISO 3452) for surface-breaking flaws in any non-porous material.
  **Magnetic particle** (BS EN ISO 9934) for surface and near-surface flaws, in ferromagnetic material only.
- **Ultrasonic** for internal flaws, thickness and bonds; **radiography** for volume flaws such as porosity; **eddy
  current** for surface flaws in conductors and in fastener holes; **CT** for the inside of complex and AM parts.
- **Specify the acceptance, not only the method:** the standard, the sensitivity, the acceptance criteria and the
  extent. "Inspect by penetrant" has no pass or fail.
- **Design for inspection:** every method needs access. Ask how a feature will be inspected while the geometry can
  still change.

Methods, probability of detection and the flaw a life calculation assumes: [NDT](../../make/ndt/).

## Analysis that reaches the drawing

### Thermal expansion and fits at temperature

- **Expansion coefficients, µm/m·K:** Invar 36 about 1.2; carbon/epoxy 0 to 2 along the fibre; molybdenum 5;
  titanium 8.6; carbon steel 12; nickel superalloys 13; austenitic stainless 16; copper 17; aluminium 23;
  magnesium 26; most polymers 50 to 100; PTFE 100 to 135.
- **The mismatch is the problem.** An aluminium housing on a steel shaft loses about 0.11 mm of diametral
  interference for each 100 mm of diameter and each 100 K. Check every fit between two families at both temperature
  extremes.
- **Shrink fits:** the diameter changes by α·D·ΔT. A 100 mm steel ring with 0.05 mm of interference needs about 40 K
  only to close it, and more to slide on. Heat rather than freeze, and stay below the tempering temperature of a
  heat-treated part.

Fits and joints through a temperature range: [Differential expansion](../../joints/differential-expansion/); below
about 200 K the coefficient is not constant: [Cryogenic design](../../materials/cryogenic/).

```text wrap
DIMENSIONS APPLY AT 20 °C.
ASSEMBLY: HEAT ITEM 2 TO [120] °C MAX AND FIT TO ITEM 1.
```

### Tolerance stacks

- **Worst case** adds every tolerance at its limit: every part assembles, and the tolerances are tight.
  **Root-sum-square** is valid only for many independent, centred, roughly normal contributors, and accepts some
  fallout. With fewer than about five contributors, or a safety-significant stack, use worst case.
- **Draw the loop,** from one face of the gap through the parts and back. Give each contributor its sign, and turn
  each geometric control into a ± contribution, with any bonus tolerance.
- **Report the dominant contributor.** If the stack fails, first shorten the chain, change the datums or add an
  adjustment. Tighter tolerances come last.

The loop, the methods side by side, and Monte Carlo: [Tolerance stack](../tolerance-stack/).

## Bonding and springs

### Adhesive bonding

- **Choose the family:** epoxy for structural strength and temperature (paste or film); structural acrylic where the
  surface preparation is poor; polyurethane for flexibility between unlike materials; cyanoacrylate only to hold a
  part while something else cures; anaerobic for threads and cylindrical fits; silicone to seal, not to carry load.
- **Load the bond in shear,** never in peel or cleavage. Add a fillet at the ends of a lap, where peel starts.
- **Make it wider, not longer.** The adhesive carries the load near the ends of the overlap and very little in the
  middle. Past a useful overlap, about 3.7/ω, a longer lap adds almost nothing; a wider one adds in proportion. The
  **Bonded lap** tool below shows why.
- **Control the bondline,** typically 0.1 to 0.25 mm for a structural joint, with scrim cloth, glass beads or
  stand-offs.
- **Surface preparation is the joint.** Degrease, abrade or etch, degrease again, and bond within the stated time. On
  aluminium, an etch or a conversion coating is much better than abrasion alone. Keep silicone out of the bond shop:
  it cannot be cleaned off reliably.

```text wrap
BOND: ADHESIVE [SPEC], BONDLINE 0.15 ±0.05, OVERLAP [25] MIN
PREP: [ABRADE / ETCH] AND DEGREASE TO [PROCESS SPEC], BOND WITHIN [4] HRS
CURE: PER [PROCESS SPEC]. FILLET AT ENDS OF OVERLAP.
VERIFY BOND BY [NDT METHOD] TO [ACCEPTANCE]. TRAVELLER COUPON PER BATCH.
```

### Springs and preload

- **Specify the rate,** in N/mm with a tolerance, and the load at one or two stated lengths. The free length follows.
- **It must not go solid in service:** a solid spring is a column, and something else breaks. EN 13906-1 also keeps a
  minimum gap between the active coils at the shortest working length.
- **Buckling:** a free length of more than about four times the mean diameter can buckle unless it is guided on a
  rod or in a bore. How the ends are seated changes the limit.
- **Set and relaxation:** springs lose load with time, faster when hot. Ask for the set to be removed (pre-set) when
  the load must hold.
- **Materials:** patented or oil-tempered spring wire (EN 10270-1 and -2) for general use; stainless steel
  (EN 10270-3) for corrosion, with a modulus about 10 to 15% lower; X-750, A-286 or Nimonic 90 for hot springs;
  beryllium copper for conductivity or no sparks.
- **Specify it as a component,** not a shape: wire and mean diameter, free length, active and total coils, wind
  direction, end type, rate, loads at lengths, the largest solid length, material, finish and the tolerance grade
  (EN 15800).

The **Spring** tool below checks a spring to EN 13906-1 and writes its specification.

```text wrap
COMPRESSION SPRING TO EN 13906-1, MATERIAL [EN 10270-1 SH]
WIRE Ø[1.0], MEAN Ø[8.0], FREE LENGTH [25], ACTIVE COILS [8], TOTAL [10]
ENDS CLOSED AND GROUND, WIND [RH]. RATE [2.49] N/mm. F2 [24.9] N AT [15]
SOLID LENGTH [10.0] MAX. SET REMOVED. TOLERANCES TO EN 15800 GRADE [2].
```

## Calculator

**Sheet metal** draws a bent part to scale with its flat pattern, gives the bend allowance, the bend deduction and the
flat length, and checks the radius, the shortest flange and the hole distance. **Spring** checks a helical
compression spring to EN 13906-1: rate, the forces and corrected stress at two lengths, the solid length with its
minimum gaps, and buckling for the way the ends are seated. **Bonded lap** gives the shear along a bonded overlap
under load, the elastic capacity, and how little a longer overlap adds. Each tab writes its callout; **Copy callout**
copies it. The K-factor and the permissible spring stress start blank: they are your shop's and your programme's
values.

## Worked example: a bracket, a spring and a doubler

Three parts of an equipment rack: a sheet-metal bracket, the spring of a latch, and a bonded doubler. **Load worked
example** sets all three tabs.

1. **The bracket** is a channel in 1.5 mm 5251-H22: legs of 25, 40 and 25 mm to the outside sharps, R2 inside, two
   90° bends. The press-brake shop gives K = 0.38 (for this example only). Bend allowance
   (π/2)·(2 + 0.38 × 1.5) = 4.04 mm; setback (2 + 1.5) × tan 45° = 3.5 mm; bend deduction 2 × 3.5 − 4.04 = 2.96 mm.
   Flat length 25 + 40 + 25 − 2 × 2.96 = 84.07 mm. R/t = 1.33, above 1t for this alloy; the 25 mm flanges are well
   above 4t + R = 8 mm; holes stay at least 5.75 mm from each bend.
2. **The latch spring** is EN 10270-1 wire, d = 1.0 mm, D = 8 mm (index 8), 8 active coils, closed and ground ends
   (10 in total), free length 25 mm, installed at 20 mm and opened to 15 mm. Rate
   81 500 × 1⁴ / (8 × 8³ × 8) = 2.49 N/mm, so 12.4 N installed and 24.9 N open. With k = 1.17 the stress at 15 mm is
   594 MPa. Against a permissible stress of 1000 MPa (half of an Rm of 2000 MPa from the wire certificate, for this
   example only) that is 59%. The solid length is 10.0 mm and the minimum gaps add 1.57 mm, so 15 mm is clear. With
   L0/D = 3.1 and both ends seated, it cannot buckle.
3. **The doubler** bonds two 2 mm 2024-T3 sheets with an epoxy film: 25 mm wide, 25 mm overlap, bondline 0.15 mm,
   Ga = 800 MPa, lap-shear strength 35 MPa. The average-stress estimate b·L·τu gives 21.9 kN. The elastic shear lag
   gives 6.46 kN: the peak at the ends is 3.4 times the average. The useful overlap is 13.5 mm, so doubling the
   overlap to 50 mm adds 0.2%, while doubling the width to 50 mm doubles the capacity. Make the doubler wider. A
   toughened film adhesive yields at the ends and carries more than the elastic value: test the joint as it will be
   made.

## Where this stops

- **The rules of thumb are typical.** K-factors, the smallest radii, flange and hole distances depend on the press
  brake and its tooling; the shop's values govern.
- **The spring check is static.** Fatigue (the EN 13906-1 fatigue diagrams), surge and relaxation at temperature are
  not included.
- **The bonded lap is elastic and in shear only.** A single lap also bends and peels; heat and moisture weaken the
  adhesive. Test the joint as it will be made and used.
- **The guides are summaries.** Where a zone covers the subject, the zone governs.

## On the drawing

- **Complete callouts:** the standard and its issue ([Standards](../standards/)), the material with its condition, and
  the finish with its specification.
- **Values with their condition:** a torque with its lubrication, dimensions after treatment, dimensions at 20 °C.
- **The acceptance, not only the method,** for welds, castings, bonds and NDT.
- **No value that the shop must guess:** a K-factor or a flat pattern for sheet metal, a rate and loads for a spring,
  a bondline and an overlap for a bond.
