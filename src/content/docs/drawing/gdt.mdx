## Why this matters

A dimension with a ± tolerance tells the size of a feature and its approximate position. It does not tell how flat,
round, square or coaxial the feature must be, or where to measure it from. Each inspector, machinist and supplier then
makes a different assumption. Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) removes this problem. It controls each
feature relative to **datums**, which you select from how the part fits and operates. The tolerance zones have the shape
that the function needs.

On a motor, GD&T can decide if a seal keeps its squeeze, if all 30 bolts of a closure start, and if a nozzle is on the
axis. It also decreases cost. It gives back the tolerance that the function does not need, and it often does this
automatically as features move away from their tightest size.

<Analogy title="Darts">
  The score of a dart depends on its distance from the bull, not on separate distances to the left and up. A ± tolerance
  on x and y is a square box. It rejects a dart in the corner of the circle that would have scored, and it accepts no
  dart that it must reject. A position tolerance is the circle. For the same worst case, it gives 57% more area.
</Analogy>

## The geometric characteristics

<GdtSymbols />

Two rules help you select between them. First, use the **smallest control that makes sure of the function**: flatness
where a face only seals, perpendicularity where it must also be square to an axis, and profile where it must also be in
the correct position. Second, use controls that **a gauge or a measuring machine can check against the same datums
that the assembly uses**.

## The feature control frame

<GdtFrame />

You read the frame as a sentence: *the position of these holes must be in a ⌀0.5 cylinder at MMC, relative to datums
A, B at MMB, and C.* These are the modifiers that are most frequently important on motor hardware:

- **Ⓜ︎ Maximum material condition.** The tolerance increases as the feature moves away from its tightest size. Use it
  for clearance: bolt holes, and pilots that only have to assemble.
- **Ⓛ Least material condition.** The opposite. Use it to protect a minimum wall or edge distance.
- **No modifier: regardless of feature size.** This is the default. Use it for features that locate accurately.
- **Ⓟ Projected tolerance zone.** The zone extends above a threaded hole, where the fastener is.
- **Ⓕ Free state.** Use it for non-rigid parts that you measure without restraint.

Build a frame below. The builder draws it, checks it against the rules of ASME Y14.5-2018 (for example: no datums on a
form tolerance, no ⌀ on runout, MMC only on a feature of size), tells where ISO is different, and gives the frame as
text for a drawing note or a CAD field.

## Datums

A **datum feature** is a real surface on the part. The **datum** is the perfect plane, axis or point that it gives in
the gauge or fixture that touches it (the datum feature simulator). Three datums in order of precedence make a **datum
reference frame**:

- **Primary.** The part sits on it at three points, which fixes three degrees of freedom. It is usually the largest
  mating face.
- **Secondary.** Two points, or the axis of a pilot, which fix two more degrees of freedom.
- **Tertiary.** One point, or a clocking hole, which fixes the last degree of freedom.

Select datums from **assembly and function**, not from what is easy in the machine shop. Examples are the face that a
closure sits on, the pilot that centres it, and the dowel that clocks it. If a datum feature has a loose control, all
the features that refer to it are also loose. Thus the datum features get their own form controls (flatness of A, and
perpendicularity of B to A). If a secondary datum such as a pilot is at **maximum material boundary (MMB)**, the pattern
can also shift by the clearance of the pilot. This tolerance is only applicable if the mating part really has that
clearance.

## Size and form

- **ASME: Rule #1.** If the drawing does not say differently, a feature of size must have perfect form at MMC. The size
  limits also limit the form (the envelope principle).
- **ISO: independency.** Under ISO 8015, size and form are independent unless the envelope requirement Ⓔ is applied.
  The same drawing can have different meanings in the two systems. Tell which system applies.
- **Non-rigid parts.** A thin case wall is not round in the free state. It becomes round when the closure pilot goes
  in. Control it in the condition where it operates: an average diameter (AVG) and circularity in the restrained
  condition, with free-state limits marked Ⓕ, and a note that tells how to restrain it for inspection (ASME Y14.5;
  ISO 10579).

## MMC, bonus tolerance and virtual condition

When a feature of size has a control at MMC, the permitted tolerance is the stated value plus the **bonus**. The bonus
is the distance of the actual size from MMC:

$$
t_{allowed} = t + |\,d_{actual} - d_{MMC}\,|
$$

The measured position from coordinate deviations is $2\sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2}$. The **virtual condition** is
the worst-case boundary that the mating part must clear:

$$
VC_{hole} = MMC - t
$$

$$
VC_{pin} = MMC + t
$$

Thus a functional gauge is a set of pins at the virtual condition of the holes, on the datums. For fastener patterns,
$F$ is the largest fastener diameter and $H$ is the clearance hole at MMC:

- **Floating fasteners** (clearance holes in the two parts): each part can have $T = H - F$.
- **Fixed fasteners** (one part has the thread): the two parts share the tolerance, $T_1 + T_2 = H - F$. Put a
  **projected tolerance zone** on the threaded holes. Without it, a tilted thread moves the fastener further over the
  height $P$ that it projects. The hole must then increase to $H = F + T_1 + T_2\,(1 + 2P/D)$, where $D$ is the minimum
  thread engagement.

## Tolerancing motor hardware

| Feature | Control | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Case bore at a seal | AVG diameter; circularity restrained | The squeeze depends on the bore as the closure holds it |
| Case flange face | Flatness; perpendicularity to the case axis | Seal seating; closure squareness |
| Case flange threaded holes | Position at MMC, projected zone, to face, axis and clocking | Bolts must start and sit without bending |
| Closure seating face (datum A) | Flatness | Primary datum and face-seal land |
| Closure pilot (datum B) | Size; perpendicularity to A | Centres the closure and its seal groove |
| Closure bolt holes | Position at MMC to A, B at MMB, C | Assembly; gauged with pins |
| Seal groove root | Circular runout or position to B | Eccentricity decreases the squeeze |
| Groove walls | Parallelism or profile | Gland volume and ring stability |
| Domes, nozzle contour | Profile of a surface to the case datums | Shape, thickness and position together |
| Ports and bosses | Position, and perpendicularity of their seal faces | Fitting alignment and sealing |

All features that the drawing does not control get the **general tolerances** in the title block (ISO 22081, or the
older ISO 2768-2). These tolerances are coarse. Class K of ISO 2768-2 permits 0.2 mm of runout on any round feature.
Make sure that each feature that the function needs has its own control.

## ASME and ISO

| Topic | ASME Y14.5-2018 | ISO GPS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size and form | Rule #1: envelope by default | Independency by default (ISO 8015); Ⓔ for envelope |
| Concentricity, symmetry | Removed in 2018 | Still used |
| Exact dimensions | Basic dimensions | Theoretically exact dimensions |
| No modifier means | Regardless of feature size | The same |
| Datum modifiers | MMB, LMB on datum references | Ⓜ︎, Ⓛ on datum references (ISO 2692) |
| Non-rigid parts | Free state, restrained condition, AVG | ISO 10579 |

## Worked example: the aft closure, its bolts and the nozzle seal groove

The starting values in each tab of the calculator are the values of this example.

1. **Datums for the closure.** The 30-bolt aft closure ([Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/#worked-example-the-aft-closure))
   sits on the case flange, and a pilot in the case bore centres it. Thus: **A** is the seating face, with its own
   flatness. **B** is the pilot diameter, perpendicular to A. **C** is a dowel hole for clocking.
2. **The bolt holes.** M10 bolts go in ⌀11.0–11.2 holes on a basic 285 mm bolt circle, with position ⌀0.5 at MMC to
   A, B at MMB, C. A hole made at ⌀11.15 gets a 0.15 bonus, so ⌀0.65 is permitted. Its axis is 0.18 mm and 0.21 mm from
   true position, which is ⌀0.553. The hole conforms **only because of the bonus**. The same error in a ⌀11.0 hole
   would be rejected, and this is correct, because a bolt possibly could not go through. The functional gauge has 30
   pins at the virtual condition, ⌀10.5.
3. **The threaded holes.** The bolts go through the 24 mm closure into threads in the case flange, so they are fixed
   fasteners. The clearance is $11.0 - 10.0 = 1.0$. If the closure holes get ⌀0.5, the threaded holes also get ⌀0.5,
   **with a projected tolerance zone 24 mm high**. Without this zone and with 15 mm of thread engagement, the threaded
   holes can have only ⌀0.12. Alternatively, the closure holes must increase to ⌀12.6, and the bolt heads and the seal
   land do not have space for this.
4. **The nozzle seal groove.** This is the radial seal of the [O-ring page](../../seals/o-ring-design/#worked-example-a-nozzle-to-case-radial-seal):
   a 150 mm bore, a pilot of ⌀149.860–149.900 and a groove root of ⌀141.45–141.55. That page assumed that the groove is
   coaxial with its pilot. It found a minimum squeeze of 10.8% at the end of life, which is just above its 10% minimum.
   The drawing must make this assumption true. With a **circular runout of 0.05 for the groove root to B**, the worst
   gland increases from 4.408 to 4.433 mm, and the same analysis gives **10.4%**. If the drawing gives no runout, the
   general tolerance permits 0.2. The gland is then 4.508 mm and the squeeze is **9.2%**. This is below the minimum, on
   a part that passes all the dimensions on the drawing.
5. **The case.** A 1.377 mm wall on a 300 mm bore is not round until the pilot goes in. Specify the bore at the seal as
   an average diameter, with circularity in the restrained condition. A plug gauge restrains the bore in place of the
   closure pilot. Also specify the flange face as perpendicular to the case axis, because it carries datum B of the
   closure.

## On the drawing

- **The applicable standard** (ASME Y14.5-2018 or ISO GPS) in the title block.
- **Datum features** selected from the assembly, each with its own form control.
- **Basic dimensions** for each true position and profile.
- **A geometric control on each feature that the function needs.** Do not leave one to the general tolerances by
  accident.
- **Projected zones** on threaded holes and press-fit pins.
- **Restraint notes** for non-rigid features, which tell how to hold the part for inspection.
