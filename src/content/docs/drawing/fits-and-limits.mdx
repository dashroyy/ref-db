## Why this matters

Each pilot, seal bore, bearing seat, dowel and bushing on a motor is a fit. If the fit is too loose, a closure can sit
off-centre, a seal can extrude, or a pin does not locate. If the fit is too tight, a part does not assemble, a bearing
gets hot, or a hub cracks. A fit selected at 20 °C can also cause these problems at −40 °C or +60 °C if the two parts
are made of different materials.

ISO 286 gives the selection as a short code, for example *⌀150 H8/f7*. All machinists, inspectors and suppliers read
this code in the same way, and it sets the two limits of the two parts.

<Analogy title="A door in its frame">
  A door needs clearance to open, but not so much that it rattles or lets cold air in. A good carpenter fits a door for
  winter and for summer, because wood swells when it is damp. You select a fit in the same way: the gap that the
  function needs, between limits that the workshop can hold, in all the conditions that the part will see.
</Analogy>

## The system

A size has a **nominal size**, a **letter** and a **grade**:

- **The grade (IT1–IT18)** is the width of the tolerance zone. It increases with size and by approximately 1.6 times
  for each grade, which is ten times for each five grades. IT6–IT7 is precision turning and grinding, IT8–IT9 is good
  turning, and IT11 and coarser is general machining.
- **The letter** is the **fundamental deviation**. It sets the position of the zone relative to the nominal size. Upper
  case letters are for holes and lower case letters are for shafts. **H** holes and **h** shafts start exactly at the
  nominal size. Shafts a–g are below it (clearance) and shafts k–z are above it (interference). js and JS are on the two
  sides of it.
- **Hole basis** is the usual selection: all holes are H and the shafts change. Reamers and plug gauges have fixed
  sizes, and it is easy to turn a shaft to any size. Shaft basis (h) is applicable to bar stock, or to a pin that goes
  through several holes.

Standard tolerances, µm, for some sizes and grades:

| Size (mm) | IT6 | IT7 | IT8 | IT9 | IT11 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| over 18 to 30 | 13 | 21 | 33 | 52 | 130 |
| over 80 to 120 | 22 | 35 | 54 | 87 | 220 |
| over 120 to 180 | 25 | 40 | 63 | 100 | 250 |
| over 250 to 315 | 32 | 52 | 81 | 130 | 320 |

## Clearance, transition and interference

<FitZones />

The hole has the limits $EI$ and $ES$, and the shaft has the limits $ei$ and $es$:

$$
c_{max} = ES - ei
$$

$$
c_{min} = EI - es
$$

If the two values are positive, the fit is a **clearance** fit. If the two values are negative, it is an
**interference** fit. If one is positive and one is negative, it is a **transition** fit, which can assemble either
way. Some usual fits, all on an H hole:

| Fit | Kind | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H11/c11, H9/d9 | Loose and free running | Coarse clearance, hot or dirty assemblies |
| H8/f7 | Close running | Running shafts, piston-type seal housings (ISO 3601) |
| H7/g6 | Sliding | Accurate location that you can still assemble by hand |
| H7/h6 | Locational clearance | Close location of parts that do not move |
| H7/k6 | Locational transition | Accurate location, assembled with light taps |
| H7/n6 | Locational transition, tighter | Location that must not move |
| H7/p6 | Locational interference | Rigid location, removed with a press |
| H7/s6 | Medium drive | Bushings and sleeves that carry load by grip |

## Fits for motor hardware

| Feature | Typical fit | What decides it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closure pilot in the case bore | H8/f7 to H7/g6 | Location and datum shift ([Datums & position](../datums-and-position/)); assembly over seals |
| Radial O-ring seal bore and plug | H8/f7 (ISO 3601-2) or closer | The largest clearance is the extrusion gap; eccentricity decreases the squeeze |
| Rolling-bearing seats | Shaft k5–m6, housing H7–J7, from the bearing manufacturer | Which ring turns under the load; bearing rings have their own tolerances (ISO 492) |
| Dowel pins | m6 pin in H7 on the fixed side, and a looser hole on the removable side | Location without assembly load on the pin |
| Bushings in lugs and clevises | H7/p6 to H7/s6 | Grip against rotation and push-out; hub stress |
| Throat inserts and liners | Designed gaps, not a standard fit | Growth during firing. See [Transient thermal](../../motor/transient-thermal/) |

## Temperature changes the fit

ISO 286 limits apply at **20 °C** (ISO 1). When two parts are made of different materials, their fit changes with
temperature:

$$
\Delta c = -\,d\,(\alpha_{shaft} - \alpha_{hole})\,(T - 20)
$$

- **An aluminium part in steel** gets tighter when hot and looser when cold. A sliding fit can seize when hot. An
  interference fit can become loose when cold if the aluminium is inside, or when hot if the aluminium is outside.
- **Parts of the same material** keep their fit at a uniform temperature. Only transients change it.
- **Interference fits** give a contact pressure, a hub stress and a holding force, from the Lamé thick-cylinder
  solution. See [Differential expansion](../../joints/differential-expansion/#fits-that-change-with-temperature).
  Check the stress at the tightest condition and the grip at the loosest condition.
- **Shrink fitting** heats the outer part (or cools the inner part) by
  $\Delta T = (\delta_{max} + c_{assembly})/(\alpha\,d)$, so that it slides on. It then grips when the temperatures
  become equal.

## Worked example: four fits on the motor

The starting values of the calculator are the first and the last of these fits.

1. **The nozzle seal bore.** The bore on the [O-ring page](../../seals/o-ring-design/#worked-example-a-nozzle-to-case-radial-seal),
   ⌀150.000–150.063, is a ⌀150 **H8**. Its plug, ⌀149.860–149.900, gives a clearance of up to 0.203 mm. This is why
   that page needed a back-up ring. The ISO 3601 housing fit, **H8/f7**, gives 0.043–0.146 mm. This is less than the
   approximately 0.15 mm that a 90 Shore ring can tolerate at 10 MPa, but more than the 0.08 mm of a 70 Shore ring.
   **H7/g6** gives 0.014–0.079 mm, which is less than the two limits. It also halves the eccentricity that decreases
   the squeeze ([GD&T](../gdt/#worked-example-the-aft-closure-its-bolts-and-the-nozzle-seal-groove)). The cost is IT6
   and IT7 machining, and a careful lead-in chamfer to assemble over the ring.
2. **An aluminium plug.** In the steel case bore, an H7/g6 fit at +60 °C becomes **0.053 mm of interference**, and the
   nozzle seizes. At −40 °C the clearance increases to **0.179 mm**, which is more than two times the extrusion gap.
   Titanium has the opposite effect, and it still becomes slightly tight when cold. A close sliding fit needs parts of
   the same material.
3. **A dowel pin.** An ⌀8 m6 pin in an H7 hole gives −0.015 to +0.009 mm. This is a transition fit, and the pin goes in
   with a push or a light press on the fixed side. On the removable side, F7 gives −0.002 to +0.022 mm, which is almost
   a slip fit.
4. **The aluminium ring on the steel attach ring.** This is the ⌀310 joint from
   [Differential expansion](../../joints/differential-expansion/#worked-example-the-300-mm-motor-from-40-to-60-c),
   now as an **H7/s6** fit. At 20 °C the interference is 0.118–0.202 mm. At −40 °C it is 0.324–0.408 mm, which gives
   4.4 MPa of contact pressure and 73 MPa in the aluminium (15% of its yield). At +60 °C it is −0.020 to +0.064 mm:
   **the fit can become loose**. That page had the same conclusion: the fit only centres the ring, and pins or bolts
   must carry the load. To assemble it, heat the ring to approximately 35 K above the steel.

## General tolerances (ISO 2768)

Most dimensions on a drawing have no tolerance next to them. A general tolerance in the title block applies to these
dimensions. ISO 2768-1 gives the linear and angular tolerances in four classes: f (fine), m (medium), c (coarse) and
v (very coarse). ISO 2768-2 gives straightness, flatness, perpendicularity, symmetry and circular runout in three
classes: H, K and L. The note "ISO 2768-mK" applies class m of part 1 and class K of part 2.

- **What it does not cover.** Part 2 gives no roundness or cylindricity: the size tolerance limits them. Parallelism
  is the larger of the size tolerance and the straightness or flatness value.
- **The envelope.** The suffix -E ("ISO 2768-mK-E") applies the envelope requirement to all single features of size.
- **Broken edges** (external radii and chamfer heights) have their own, larger tolerances.
- **ISO 22081 replaced ISO 2768-2.** It gives a general profile tolerance to stated datums instead of the H, K and L
  classes. Many drawings still use ISO 2768, so check which standard your programme uses.
- **It is not a reason to stop tolerancing.** Give each functional dimension its own tolerance. Make sure that the shop
  can hold the general class on all the other dimensions, because each one is a requirement.

## Where this stops

- **ISO 286 tables to 500 mm** for the usual letters (d to s, D to S) and grades IT4–IT12. Larger sizes, other letters
  and J holes are in the standard.
- **Uniform temperature.** Firing transients heat parts unevenly. Get the temperatures from
  [Transient thermal](../../motor/transient-thermal/).
- **Elastic interference fits,** with no smoothing of the surfaces, no centrifugal loosening and no creep.
- **Form is separate.** Under ISO 8015, a size tolerance does not control form. Apply the envelope requirement Ⓔ to
  fitting features, or use the ASME Rule #1 ([GD&T](../gdt/#size-and-form)).

## On the drawing

- **The fit as limits or as a code:** ⌀150 H8 and ⌀150 f7, or the limits, on the drawing of each part.
- **The envelope requirement** on fitting features under ISO GPS (⌀150 H8 Ⓔ).
- **Surface finish** on press fits and shrink fits ([Surface finish](../surface-finish/#bearing-seats-and-fits)), and lead-in chamfers where a part or a seal slides on.
- **The assembly method** for interference fits: press, heat or cool, and the temperatures.
- **Parts of the same material,** or the temperature range for which the fit was checked.
