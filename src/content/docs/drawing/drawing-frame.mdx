## Why this matters

The views and dimensions of a drawing show the shape of a part. The title block and the general notes tell the reader
how to interpret that shape: which standard sets the meaning of each symbol, which projection the views use, which
tolerance applies where none is shown, and which processes the part needs. A supplier reads them first, and an
inspector checks the part against them.

A wrong or vague entry here affects every dimension at once. A drawing to ISO read as ASME, or a first-angle drawing
read as third angle, gives a different part from correct views. A note such as "break all sharp edges" cannot be
inspected, so it gives a dispute instead of a requirement.

<Analogy title="The rules before the game">
  Two teams can play the same match under different rules and both think that they won. A drawing is the same: the
  title block states the rules. If it does not, the supplier and the inspector use the rules they know, and they can
  both be right about a part that does not fit.
</Analogy>

## What the title block must state

| Field | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Governing standard and issue | Sets how every symbol is read. ISO GPS and ASME Y14.5 give different meanings to some of the same symbols |
| Projection symbol | First angle in the UK and Europe, third angle in the US. Never assume |
| Units | Millimetres unless stated. State it anyway |
| General tolerances | Apply to every dimension that has no tolerance of its own |
| Default surface texture | Applies to every surface without its own symbol ([Surface finish](../surface-finish/)) |
| Material, specification and condition | Alloy, condition (including heat treatment), product form and specification ([Material specs](../../materials/specification/)) |
| Criticality class | Sets the inspection and the approvals ([Criticality](../../fundamentals/criticality/)) |
| Export-control marking | A legal requirement where it applies |
| Mass, scale, sheet *n* of *m*, revision | Housekeeping that auditors check ([Revision & sign-off](../revision-and-sign-off/)) |

## ISO and ASME read size differently

- **ISO 8015: independency.** A size tolerance controls only the local sizes, not the form. A shaft can be in size and
  still be bent. Where form must stay inside the size, add the envelope requirement Ⓔ to the feature, or to all
  features with "ISO 2768-mK-E".
- **ASME Y14.5: Rule #1.** Size controls form: a feature at maximum material condition must have perfect form (the
  envelope). A note can remove this where it is not needed.

Thus the same drawing can give two different parts. The governing standard and its issue in the title block decide
which one is correct. Taylor's principle on the [Gauging page](../gauging/#limit-gauges) shows how each one is checked.

## General tolerances

- **ISO 2768** gives linear and angular tolerances (classes f, m, c, v) in part 1, and geometric tolerances (classes
  H, K, L) in part 2. The [lookup on the Fits page](../fits-and-limits/#general-tolerances-iso-2768) gives the values.
  ISO 22081 has replaced part 2, but many drawings still use it. Check which your programme uses.
- **Title-block tolerances** (ASME practice) give a tolerance by the number of decimal places, for example ".XX ±0.01".
- **General tolerances are for features without a function.** Each functional feature (a seal groove, a pilot, a bore,
  a hole pattern) needs its own tolerance. A general tolerance on a functional feature is a requirement that nobody
  decided.

## Notes that work

Each general note is a requirement. It must be specific (it gives a value, a level or a class), verifiable (an
inspector can check it) and stated once. These shapes are worth making standard. Fill the brackets from your
programme's specifications:

- Interpret drawing per [governing standard and issue].
- Remove burrs. Break sharp edges [range] mm unless otherwise specified.
- Dimensions apply [before / after] surface treatment.
- Mask threads and surfaces marked [symbol] from treatment.
- Tighten to [torque] N·m with [lubricant]; friction coefficient assumed [range].
- Bake for hydrogen embrittlement relief per [specification] within [time] of plating.
- Mark part and serial number per [specification] at location shown, by [method]. No impression stamping on [class]
  parts.
- Clean to [cleanliness level] per [specification] before assembly.
- Inspect [features] by [method] per [standard] to [acceptance standard and class].

## Notes that do not work

| Note | Problem |
| --- | --- |
| "Break all sharp edges" | No size: an inspector cannot accept or reject an edge |
| "Clean before assembly" | No level and no method |
| "Finish to suit" | Gives a design decision to the shop |
| "Torque to standard values" | Which standard? Which lubricant? |
| "Torque to 60 N·m" | No lubricant: the same torque gives very different preloads ([Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/)) |
| "Wall approximately 1.4 mm" | An approximate value cannot be inspected. Give a tolerance, or mark the value as reference |
| A torque in a note and a different torque on a view | A nonconformance whichever one the shop follows |

## Worked example: the aft closure drawing

The aft closure of the 300 mm motor is D6AC, bolted to the case with 30 M10 bolts
([Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/)), with the nozzle seal bore of the
[O-ring page](../../seals/o-ring-design/). Select **Load worked example** to put this part in the builder.

1. **How it is read.** ISO GPS, first-angle projection, millimetres. Under ISO 8015, the seal bore is not inside its
   envelope by default. It needs Ⓔ, or a cylindricity tolerance, because the O-ring squeeze depends on its form.
2. **Defaults.** ISO 2768-mK for the features without a function, Ra 3.2 µm, and edge breaks of 0.2–0.5 mm. The seal
   bore, the pilot, the bolt pattern and the seal faces all have their own tolerances and finishes
   ([GD&T](../gdt/#worked-example-the-aft-closure-its-bolts-and-the-nozzle-seal-groove)).
3. **Plating.** The closure is zinc-nickel plated. D6AC at 1520 MPa is far above 1000 MPa, so the builder adds the
   hydrogen-embrittlement relief bake. It also adds the notes for the dimensions before or after plating and for
   masking: the seal bore and the threads stay bare.
4. **Bolts.** The torque note has a bracket for the torque and for the lubricant. The bolted-joints page gives 60 N·m
   with a friction coefficient of 0.12. Write both on the drawing, or the preload is not defined.
5. **Inspection and marking.** The closure is a critical pressure part: magnetic particle inspection per ASTM E1444
   with an acceptance class, electrolytic etch marking, and no impression stamping.

The builder gives 13 notes, with 22 blanks to fill in the title block and the notes. Now select **Check your notes**. The default text is the list of
notes that do not work: the checker rejects 8 of the 9, and finds the two different torque values. Only "Tighten to
55 N·m with MoS2 grease" passes, and it conflicts with note 6.

## Where this stops

- **The shape of each note,** not your company's wording or its specification numbers. Use your drawing standard.
- **Word patterns.** The checker finds the usual faults. It cannot tell if a specification is the correct one, or if a
  value is correct.
- **No sheet layout.** Border, zones, revision table and parts list follow your drawing standard (ISO 7200, ASME Y14.1).

## On the drawing

- **The title block complete,** with the governing standard and its issue, and the projection symbol.
- **Every functional feature toleranced explicitly.** General tolerances only for features without a function.
- **Each requirement once.** If a value is on a view, do not repeat it in a note.
- **Every note verifiable:** a value, a level, a class, a standard.
