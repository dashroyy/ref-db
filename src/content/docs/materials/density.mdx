## Why this matters

Every gram of case, insulation, nozzle and hardware is a gram of propellant the motor does not carry, so mass is the
measure that most design choices are judged by. The mass of a part comes from its volume and its density, and the
density is the number that most often goes wrong. It is not usually a wrong value: it is a right value in the wrong
units.

A finite-element model with its density 1000 times too high still runs. Its stresses under pressure are correct, so
nothing looks wrong. But its natural frequencies are 31.6 times too low, and its gravity, inertia and shock loads 1000
times too high.

<Analogy title="A recipe in the wrong units">
  A recipe that says "200" for the flour works in grams and fails in ounces. Nothing in the kitchen tells you the
  units are wrong: the cake just comes out as a brick. A density in a model is the same. The number is correct; only
  the units decide whether the result is.
</Analogy>

## Units: read this once

| Unit system | Density unit | Steel (7.85 g/cm³) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| m, kg, s, N, Pa (SI) | kg/m³ | 7850 |
| mm, tonne, s, N, MPa (most mm models) | t/mm³ | 7.85 × 10⁻⁹ |
| mm, kg, ms, kN, GPa (LS-DYNA) | kg/mm³ | 7.85 × 10⁻⁶ |
| in, lbf, s, psi | lbf·s²/in⁴ | 7.35 × 10⁻⁴ |

- **The mm–N–MPa system needs tonnes.** A newton is a kilogram-metre per second squared; in millimetres it is a
  tonne-millimetre per second squared. So the density is in t/mm³: 10⁻⁹ times the value in g/cm³. Typing the kg/mm³
  value (10⁻⁶ times) makes the model 1000 times too heavy.
- **lb/in³ is a weight, not a mass.** In an in–lbf–s model, divide it by g = 386.09 in/s².
- **Check the total mass** of every model against a hand calculation before you trust a frequency or an inertia load.

## What changes the density

- **Alloying, a little.** Tempers and heat treatments hardly change it. Lithium is the exception: Al-Li alloys are
  about 4–8% lighter than the 2xxx alloys they replace.
- **Fibre volume fraction** in a composite, and **filler** in a polymer. Filled polymers are sold by weight percent:
  blend the specific volumes, not the densities: $1/\rho = w/\rho_f + (1 - w)/\rho_m$.
- **Porosity** in castings, additive parts and powder-metallurgy parts, and the voids of a moulding.
- **Cores and foams** are sold by density: the core density sets its stiffness and strength.
- **Temperature.** Volume grows by about three times the linear expansion; the density falls by the same fraction.

## Worked example: the case, its model and a bracket

Select **Load worked example**.

1. **The mass of the case.** One metre of the 300 mm D6AC case with its 1.377 mm wall
   ([Case sizing](../../motor/case-sizing/)) is 1.304 litres of steel: **10.23 kg**. The same dimensions in
   filament-wound carbon/epoxy are 2.05 kg. That is not the mass of a composite case: its wall must be thicker for the
   same burst pressure. Compare at equal strength ([Material selection](../selection/)).
2. **The density in the FE model.** The case model is in mm, N and MPa, so D6AC goes in as **7.85 × 10⁻⁹ t/mm³**. If the
   kg/mm³ value (7.85 × 10⁻⁶) is typed instead, the model is 1000 times too heavy. Its stresses under pressure stay
   correct, but its first mode falls to 3.16% of the true frequency, and its gravity and acceleration loads rise
   1000 times. The total mass check (10.23 kg per metre against the model) finds this at once.
3. **A filled bracket.** PEEK with 30% chopped carbon fibre by weight is **1.418 g/cm³**; with 30% glass fibre it is
   1.524 g/cm³, 7% heavier for the same weight percent. A linear blend of the densities would give 1.45 and 1.68 g/cm³:
   too heavy, by 2% and 10%.

## Where this stops

- **Typical densities** at room temperature. The certificate or the datasheet of the actual material governs.
- **Void-free filled polymers.** Real mouldings and composites have some porosity: measure the density
  (ASTM D792, ISO 1183) where the mass matters.
- **Simple shapes only** in the mass tab. For a real part, take the volume from CAD and enter it.

## On the drawing

- **The mass** of the part, with its tolerance where it matters, and how it is checked.
- **The centre of gravity** of heavy parts and assemblies, for handling and for the mass properties of the motor.
- **The density basis** of a composite or a filled part: the fibre volume fraction or the grade, not only the resin.

## References and further reading

- ASTM D792 and ISO 1183, *Density of plastics by displacement*.
- ASTM D3171, *Constituent content of composite materials* (fibre volume fraction).
- MMPDS, *Metallic Materials Properties Development and Standardization* (densities of aerospace alloys).
