## Why this matters

The first job on a new part is not geometry. It is to turn the specification into a list of numbered requirements,
each with the method that will prove it. A requirement with no number cannot be met or failed; a requirement with
no verification method will be argued about at the end, when changes are most expensive.

Most problems found at integration are at interfaces: a bolt that one team thought the other would supply, a load
given at a point that nobody stated, a seal face that both sides thought the other would finish. These are boundaries
between teams, and an interface control document (ICD) is how they are managed.

<Analogy title="A kitchen brief">
  Ask a builder for "a good kitchen with enough storage" and you will get the builder's idea of good and enough. Ask
  for "worktops 900 mm high, 4 m of wall cupboards, a 600 mm oven housing", and you can check each one with a tape
  measure. The second brief can be built, priced and accepted. A requirement is the same.
</Analogy>

## The compliance matrix

Convert the specification into a compliance matrix: one row for each requirement, each with how it will be proven.

| Method | Use for | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test | Anything with a pass or fail that you can measure under load | Test report against a released procedure |
| Analysis | Strength, stiffness, thermal, where a test is impractical | Analysis report with margins |
| Inspection | Dimensions, markings, finish, material | Inspection record, certificate |
| Demonstration | Assembly, access, operation | Witnessed record |

Each row also has the parent requirement it comes from, the owner, the evidence and its status. The matrix is the
plan for verification, and at the end it is the proof.

## Words that make a requirement unverifiable

| Wording | Problem | Rewrite as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "suitable", "adequate", "robust" | No threshold | The number it must meet |
| "minimise", "maximise" | No end point | A limit, plus a goal if wanted |
| "as required", "where necessary" | Delegates the decision | The specific condition |
| "and/or" | Two requirements in one | Two rows |
| "should", "may" | Not binding in most specifications | "shall", or move it to guidance |
| Two "shall" in one sentence | Two requirements in one | One row each |

**Track every TBD and TBC** in the same matrix, with an owner and a date. A TBD that reaches drawing release becomes
whatever the designer assumed on the day.

## Derived requirements

Most of what governs a detail part is derived, not stated. A leak requirement becomes a seal groove, a surface
finish, a flatness and a squeeze range ([O-ring design](../../seals/o-ring-design/)). A life requirement becomes an NDT
method and a proof factor ([NDT](../../make/ndt/)). Write the derived requirements down and trace each to its parent.
When the parent changes, you can then find every feature that it drives.

## The interface control document

<IcdBoundary />

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Geometry | Mating features and the datum reference frame that both sides share |
| Tolerance split | How the allowable mismatch is divided between the two parts ([Tolerance stack](../../drawing/tolerance-stack/)) |
| Loads | Forces and moments across the interface, both directions, all load cases, at a stated point |
| Thermal | Temperature on each side, heat flow, allowable differential growth |
| Sealing | Which side has the groove, which the face; finish and flatness |
| Fasteners | Who supplies them; size, grade, torque and lubricant |
| Bonding and earthing | Electrical continuity requirement and surface preparation |
| Freeze date | When it stops changing, and the change route after that |

**Design to the ICD, not to the other team's current model.** It feels efficient until they move a hole. The ICD is
the only document that both teams can be held to, and each change goes through it with both signatures.

## Interface loads

A load set without its reference point is incomplete. The same force at a different point has a different moment:
moving a load set $(\mathbf{F}, \mathbf{M}_A)$ from point $A$ to point $B$ gives

$$
\mathbf{M}_B = \mathbf{M}_A + (\mathbf{r}_A - \mathbf{r}_B) \times \mathbf{F}
$$

with the same force. For a circular pattern of $n$ bolts on diameter $D$, the axial force divides equally, and a
bending moment $M$ adds at most $4M/(nD)$ to the most loaded bolt. Then add the preload and check the joint
([Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/)).

## Budgets and margins

Mass, power, leak rate and tolerances are shared quantities. A budget allocates each to the items that use it and
keeps a reserve at the system level.

- **Values grow as designs mature.** A sketch is light; the part that is made is heavier. A growth allowance on each
  item, by its maturity (a few per cent for a measured part, 20% or more for a layout), predicts the final value.
  AIAA S-120 and ISO 22010 give such allowances for mass.
- **The reserve belongs to the system owner,** not to any item. It is released by a decision.
- **Track the predicted margin,** not the current one. A budget that fits with current values and fails with the
  growth allowances needs action now, while changes are cheap.

## Worked example: the nozzle interface of the 300 mm motor

The motor team owns the aft closure; a supplier makes the nozzle. The first draft of their ICD has six requirements.
The load set, the bolt pattern, the growth allowances, the 30 kg allowable inert mass and the 1.5 kg reserve are **for
this example only**. Push **Load worked example** in the calculator to load them.

1. **The wording.** Requirement 1, "suitable for the motor environment", has no threshold: rewrite it to reference
   the environment specification and its revision. Requirement 3, "should be minimised", has no binding verb and no
   end point: requirement 6 (6.0 kg or less, verified by weighing) replaces it. Requirement 4, "bolts and/or pins ...
   as required", is two requirements and delegates the decision: split it into who supplies the bolts and what they
   are. Requirement 5 has a TBD: it gets an owner and a date. Requirements 2 (the leak rate from
   [Leak testing](../../make/leak-testing/)) and 6 are verifiable by test.
2. **The loads.** The ICD gives the loads at the nozzle reference point, 120 mm aft of the interface plane: 65.6 kN
   axial (the blow-off load from [Load paths](../../joints/load-paths/#worked-example-the-300-mm-motors-load-path)) and
   0.88 kN lateral (15 g on the 6 kg nozzle). At the interface plane, this is a bending moment of **106 N·m**.
3. **The bolts.** With 12 bolts on a 150 mm circle, the axial share is 5.47 kN for each bolt and the bending adds
   0.24 kN: **5.70 kN** on the most loaded bolt, 4% more. Here the moment is small. A load set with no stated point
   could have hidden a large one.
4. **The budget.** The inert mass is 26.61 kg today ([Rocket motor hardware](../../motor/hardware/)): the case 11.77 kg
   (calculated, 5% growth), the insulation 4.84 kg and the nozzle 6 kg (estimated, 15%), and other hardware 4 kg (a
   layout, 25%). With the current values, the margin is **+1.89 kg**. With the growth allowances, the predicted total
   is 29.82 kg and the margin is **−1.32 kg**. The budget fits today and fails at maturity: the time to act is now.

Two requirements to rewrite, one to delete, one open item, a load set with its point, and a budget that needs a
decision: each is cheap now and expensive after the drawings are released.

## Where this stops

- **The word check finds wording problems,** not wrong numbers. Every requirement still needs review by people who
  know where the value comes from.
- **Rigid-body load transfer and a rigid bolt pattern.** Flexible flanges, prying and non-circular patterns change the
  bolt loads ([Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/)).
- **One growth allowance for each item.** Real budgets track maturity, uncertainty and known changes separately.
- **No requirements management tool.** Large programmes keep requirements, traces and verification in a database.

## On the drawing

- **The requirements that the part meets,** through the specification and the ICD that the drawing references.
- **The features that come from derived requirements,** with a note or a key characteristic where the trace matters.
- **The interface datums** of the ICD, used as the datums of the drawing.
- **The ICD revision** that the drawing is designed to.
