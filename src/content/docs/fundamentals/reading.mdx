## Why this matters

Every number on this site is a screening value. Real allowables come from MMPDS, CMH-17 or the specification that
your contract invokes. Real methods come from the standards and from the books below. Use this site to find your way
and to make the list of options shorter. Then go to the source, and record the issue that you used, before anything is
released.

The sources are also where the experience is. The NASA design monographs were written by the engineers who built the
hardware, at the time when the industry learned its lessons at high cost. They say what failed, and why. A calculator
cannot tell you that.

<Analogy title="A road atlas and the survey">
  A road atlas gets you to the right town, and it shows which roads join. You do not plan a building from it: for that,
  you get the survey of the site. This site is the atlas. The sources below are the survey. They are slower to read, but
  they are what you build from.
</Analogy>

## Free libraries

| Library | What it holds | Access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NASA Technical Reports Server (ntrs.nasa.gov) | NASA reports, the SP-8000 design monographs, the Fastener Design Manual | Free |
| NASA Technical Standards System (standards.nasa.gov) | NASA-STD and NASA-HDBK documents in their current revisions | Free |
| ASSIST Quick Search (quicksearch.dla.mil) | MIL standards, specifications and handbooks | Free |
| ECSS (ecss.nl) | The European space standards | Free after registration |
| UK Defence Standardization (dstan.mod.uk) | DEF STAN documents | Free to register; some parts go only through the Design Authority |
| NIST (webbook.nist.gov, trc.nist.gov) | Fluid properties and cryogenic material properties | Free |

**Start with NTRS.** It holds the complete SP-8000 series, NASA Space Vehicle Design Criteria, free and searchable.
Each monograph states the design criteria, the recommended practice, and the failures that taught them. Search for the
SP number. This list keeps to the monographs on hardware.

## Worked example: a reading pack for a case review

A new engineer joins the team that sizes the 300 mm motor case of the other pages. Select **Reading paths**, then
**Motor case and pressure vessels**.

1. **The path has eight sources.** Four of them are free: SP-8025, SP-8040, NASA-STD-5019 and SP-8007.
2. **Read them in order.** SP-8025 gives the criteria for the case and the failures behind them. Sutton gives the
   context of the motor. Roark gives the hand checks. SP-8040 explains the proof test, and Anderson the fracture
   mechanics behind it. NASA-STD-5019 gives the requirements of a fracture control plan. SP-8007 covers the buckling of
   the empty case, and MMPDS the allowables.
3. **Push Copy the list** and paste the eight references into the induction pack.
4. **To find the source behind one number,** go to **Find a source** and search for a word. A search for "knockdown"
   finds SP-8007, the source of the 0.56 factor on the [Shell buckling](../../verify/shell-buckling/) page, and
   Timoshenko & Gere, which explains why that factor is necessary.

## Where this stops

- **A starting list, not a complete one.** The document list of your programme and your company library govern.
- **Editions change.** Check the current edition before you cite a page, a table or a clause.
- **Some sources need a licence** or have a limited distribution: MMPDS, ESDU and the JANNAF mechanical behaviour
  manual. Get them through your organisation.
- **A textbook is not a design standard.** Take the method from the textbook, and the factors and the allowables from
  your requirements.
- **The page lists come from the text.** A page can use a method without naming its source, so a source can be more
  important than its list shows.
- **The scope is the scope of the site:** hardware, structures, materials, process and inspection. Propellant
  formulation, energetics and initiation are outside it.

## In your stress report

- **Cite the source and its edition** for each method and each allowable, so that a reviewer can find what you used.
- **Use the primary source,** not this site, for any value that goes into a released analysis.
- **Keep the identity of each issue that you used** with the report. Sources change, and the analysis must stay
  traceable to the issue that it used.

## Differences from REF·DB v1

- The v1 list had 31 sources in eight groups. This list has 75 in ten subjects, with the sources that the rebuilt pages
  cite: polymers and seals, bolts, verification and environments are new.
- The pages that cite each source are found in the page text when the site is built, so the list stays current.
- New: reading paths, filters by page and by free sources, and copy buttons that give a reference list.
