## Why this matters

Fracture analysis gives the size of the flaw that fails the part. NDT is how you show that no flaw of that size is
present. The two must agree: the inspection must reliably find flaws that are smaller than the critical flaw, in the
material, orientation and location where the process makes them.

Thus the question is not "which NDT do we specify?". The question is "is the critical flaw larger than the flaw that
the inspection can miss?". If the critical flaw is smaller, more inspection does not help. Change the design, or use a
proof test to screen the flaws ([Proof testing](../../verify/proof-testing/)).

<Analogy title="A metal detector on a beach">
  You search a beach for a lost ring with a metal detector. A large ring you find nearly every time. A small ring you
  find only sometimes, and a ring under a rock you never find, however carefully you sweep. If someone asks "is there
  a ring on this beach?", the honest answer is "no ring larger than this size, in the places that I could sweep". NDT
  gives the same answer about cracks.
</Analogy>

## The methods

| Method | Finds | Blind to | Materials | Indicative depth found reliably |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visual (VT) | Damage, gross cracks, weld profile, corrosion, wrong features | Tight cracks; all flaws below the surface | All | Not a fracture-control method |
| Fluorescent penetrant (PT) | Cracks, laps and porosity open to the surface | Flaws not open to the surface; cracks smeared shut | Non-porous metals | 0.6 mm |
| Magnetic particle (MT) | Surface and slightly subsurface cracks | Non-magnetic alloys; cracks along the field | Ferromagnetic steels | 1.0 mm |
| Eddy current (ET) | Small surface and near-surface cracks, through thin paint | Deep flaws; edges and changes of geometry | Conductive metals | 0.5 mm |
| Ultrasonic (UT, PAUT) | Planar flaws in the volume: cracks, lack of fusion, laminations, delaminations | Flaws parallel to the beam; the dead zone near the surface | Most, with couplant | 0.8 mm |
| Radiography (RT, CT) | Porosity, inclusions, voids; a permanent image | Tight cracks; planar flaws across the beam | All | About 0.7 × the wall, for cracks |
| Leak test (LT) | Flaws that go through the wall | All flaws that are not yet through the wall | All | The wall |
| Acoustic emission (AE) | Cracks that grow while the part is loaded, for example in proof | Flaws that do not grow during the test | All | No size |

The depths are for a semi-elliptical surface crack five times as long as it is deep. They are of the order of the
**standard NDE flaw sizes** of NASA-STD-5009. They apply only when a qualified procedure is used on a prepared surface
by certified personnel. A smaller size needs a special demonstration on your parts.

### Every method has a blind orientation

<NdtBlind />

- **Radiography** sees a flaw by the change in the thickness that the beam goes through. A crack parallel to the beam
  shows. A planar flaw across the beam, such as lack of fusion on a weld side wall, can show almost nothing. Its
  sensitivity is approximately 2% of the thickness.
- **Ultrasonic** sees a flaw by the sound that it reflects. A flaw across the beam reflects well. A flaw parallel to
  the beam does not, which is why welds are inspected with angled beams from two directions.
- **Magnetic particle** sees a crack where it interrupts the field. Magnetise in two directions at approximately 90°.
- **Penetrant** sees only what is open to the surface. Machining, grinding, blasting and peening can smear metal over a
  crack and close it. Etch before penetrant on machined fracture-critical surfaces, and inspect before shot peening.

Choose the method for the flaw that the process makes: lack of fusion and cracks in welds, laps and seams in
forgings, porosity in castings, lack of fusion in printed parts, fatigue cracks at stress concentrations, and
delaminations in composites.

## Probability of detection

No inspection finds every flaw. The probability of detection (POD) increases with the flaw size, along an S-shaped
curve. MIL-HDBK-1823A fits a log-normal curve to the results of trials:

$$
\text{POD}(a) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln a - \mu}{\sigma}\right)
\qquad
a_{90} = e^{\,\mu + 1.282\,\sigma}
$$

Here $\Phi$ is the standard normal distribution, $\mu$ sets the position of the curve and $\sigma$ its spread. The
size usually quoted is $a_{90/95}$: the size found with 90% probability, at 95% confidence that the real curve is not
worse. Thus:

- **$a_{90/95}$ is not a size that is always found.** One flaw in ten of that size is missed. Damage tolerance therefore
  assumes that a flaw of that size is present in every part, and calculates the life from it.
- **A large spread is a bad method,** even with a good $a_{90}$: it misses some large flaws.
- **A repeat inspection by the same method helps less than you expect.** It misses for the same reasons: orientation,
  access, a tight crack. A second, different method helps more.

### Demonstration: 29 of 29

A method shows its POD by trials on real flaws of known size, inspected blind. If all of the trials find the flaw, the
lower confidence bound on POD after $n$ trials is $(1 - C)^{1/n}$. For 90% at 95% confidence, $0.05^{1/29} = 0.902$:
thus **29 found of 29** is the least that demonstrates 90/95 at one flaw size. One miss needs 46 flaws (45 found), and
two misses need 61. A full POD curve needs more trials, at several sizes, and a hit/miss or signal-response analysis
(MIL-HDBK-1823A).

The trial flaws must represent the hardware: real cracks (not machined slots), in the same material, geometry, surface
condition and access, inspected with the same procedure, equipment and people.

## Damage tolerance: the life from the flaw that NDT can miss

<NdtLife />

The fracture-control argument for a flight part:

1. Assume that a crack of the size that the inspection can miss, $a_\text{NDE}$, is present at the worst location and
   in the worst orientation.
2. Calculate its growth under the service loads, for example with the Paris law $da/dN = C\,\Delta K^m$, until it gets
   to the critical size $a_\text{crit}$.
3. The cycles between the two sizes must be at least the service life times the life factor of the programme. Flight
   pressure vessel standards usually use four.

Because the crack grows slowly at first, most of the life is spent while the crack is small. Thus a method that finds
a smaller flaw gives much more life. When no method can find a flaw sufficiently small, a proof test can set the
starting size instead ([Proof testing](../../verify/proof-testing/#the-flaw-screening-argument)).

## Preparation, access and people

- **Sequence.** Inspect after the final heat treatment, machining and straightening, and before shot peening, plating,
  painting and insulation. A repair, rework or weld after NDT needs NDT again.
- **Surface.** Penetrant needs a clean, open surface: etch after machining. Eddy current needs a smooth surface and
  reference standards of the same material and geometry. Ultrasonic needs a surface for the probe and couplant.
- **Access.** Each method sees only the surfaces it can reach. The inside of a closed motor case is reached only
  through its openings, and the inside of a bonded assembly not at all. Plan the inspection when you design the part,
  not after it is made.
- **Geometry.** Edges, threads, fillets and thickness changes give false signals or dead zones. Keep the critical
  locations of the part where a method can see them.
- **People and procedures.** NDT personnel are certified to NAS 410 or EN 4179 (levels 1 to 3). Each inspection
  follows a written procedure approved by a level 3. An inspection by uncertified people, or without a procedure, is
  not evidence.

## NDT for motor hardware

| Part | Flaws that the process makes | Usual methods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steel case, membrane | Laminations, laps, cracks from forming and heat treatment | UT of the plate or forging; MT or ET on the outside; proof test |
| Case welds | Lack of fusion, cracks, porosity, undercut | RT and UT together; MT or PT on the surface |
| Closures, bosses and forgings | Forging laps, internal bursts, machining damage | UT of the forging; PT or MT after machining |
| Threads and fasteners | Laps from rolling, cracks at the root | MT or PT; ET for rolled threads |
| Composite case and skirts | Delaminations, voids, fibre damage | UT (C-scan), CT; acoustic emission during proof |
| Nozzle parts, carbon and silica phenolic | Delaminations, voids, density variation | CT and RT; UT where the thickness permits |
| Insulation and liner bonds | Unbonded areas | UT, tap test, RT of the loaded motor |
| Printed parts | Lack of fusion, trapped powder, porosity | CT; PT after the surface is machined |

## Worked example: can NDT clear the D6AC case?

This is the case of the [proof-testing example](../../verify/proof-testing/#worked-example-the-d6ac-case): 1094 MPa
hoop stress at MEOP, a 1.377 mm wall, $K_{IC} = 70$ MPa√m, and martensitic-steel crack growth with full cycles from zero
to MEOP. Two pressurisations follow the inspection. The life factor of 4 and the 90/95 criterion are for this example
only. Push **Load worked example** in the calculator to load these inputs.

1. **The critical flaw.** For a semi-elliptical surface crack with $a/c = 0.4$ (Newman–Raju), $K$ at the deepest point
   reaches 70 MPa√m at a depth of **0.88 mm**: 64% of the wall.
2. **The methods.** With the indicative depths: eddy current (0.51 mm) gives 309 cycles to critical, penetrant
   (0.64 mm) 165 and ultrasonic (0.76 mm) 70. The requirement is $2 \times 4 = 8$. Magnetic particle (0.97 mm) and
   radiography (0.96 mm) miss flaws that are larger than critical, so they cannot clear the membrane.
3. **Access.** Eddy current and penetrant need the surface. The outside is easy, but the inside of a welded case is
   reached only through its openings. From the outside, only ultrasonic sees the inner surface, and its flaw is only
   1.15 times smaller than critical.
4. **Sensitivity.** A 10% higher stress (a local peak at a weld) brings the critical flaw to 0.78 mm, and a cold-day
   toughness of 56 MPa√m brings it to 0.66 mm. Ultrasonic then cannot clear the inner surface, and penetrant only just
   clears the outer surface. Only eddy current keeps its margin.
5. **POD.** In the **Probability of detection** tab, ultrasonic with $a_{90} = 0.76$ mm and $\sigma = 0.4$ finds a
   0.88 mm flaw 94.9% of the time. If 20 cases each have one flaw of that size, the chance that at least one is missed
   is **65%**. A second, independent inspection would bring this to 5%, but repeat inspections are not independent.
6. **Demonstration.** To use 0.76 mm on this hardware, the UT procedure must find 29 of 29 real cracks of that size
   (90.2% lower bound at 95% confidence). With 28 of 29 the bound is 84.7%, and the procedure fails.

Result: NDT alone cannot carry the fracture case of this thin, high-strength membrane. The proof test does
([Proof testing](../../verify/proof-testing/)), cold if the toughness falls at low temperature. NDT then does what it
does well: the welds (RT and UT together), the outer surface (ET or MT), and the forgings before machining.

## Where this stops

- **The method depths are indicative.** Use the sizes that your NDE procedure has demonstrated on your parts, or the
  standard sizes of your programme with their conditions.
- **One flaw shape** for the critical size and the growth. Real flaws change shape as they grow; corner cracks at holes
  and embedded flaws need their own solutions.
- **No growth during proof, no growth under sustained load,** and no environment. These can shorten the life a lot.
- **POD from one curve.** Real POD depends on the operator, the surface, the geometry and the orientation. The binomial
  demonstration is for one flaw size only.

## On the drawing

- **The method and the specification,** for example fluorescent penetrant to ASTM E1417 with the type, method and
  sensitivity level, or ultrasonic to ASTM E2375 with the class.
- **The acceptance criteria,** for example no linear indications, or an acceptance class for the welds.
- **The area,** where it is not the whole part: welds, threads, fillets, fracture-critical zones.
- **The sequence:** after final heat treatment and machining; etch before penetrant; before peening, plating and
  painting.
- **The classification** of the part, which sets the inspection level and the records ([Gauging](../../drawing/gauging/)).
