## Why this matters

- **Every loaded motor is inspected,** by radiography or computed tomography, and the inspection finds voids, cracks
  and unbonds. Someone must decide which ones a motor can fly with, and the criteria must exist before the first motor
  is cast.
- **A flaw that the flame reaches is new burning surface:** more gas and a higher pressure, amplified by $1/(1-n)$.
- **A crack can grow at ignition,** when the gas fills it and the bore stretches. More crack is more surface, more
  pressure and more growth.
- **An unbond lets the gas get behind the grain.** The insulation there is often sized for a short exposure at the end
  of the burn, and it can see the whole burn.
- **Both errors are expensive.** Criteria that are too strict scrap good motors; criteria that are too loose fly a motor
  that fails.

## The intuition: a split log

<Analogy title="A split log">
  A log with a split burns from inside the split as well as from outside: the fire has more surface, and it gets deep
  into the wood. A small knot hole deep in the log makes no difference. A grain is the same. The flaws that matter are
  the ones at the surface and at the ends; the small ones deep inside do not. Its acceptance criteria follow from where
  each flaw is.
</Analogy>

## The flaws

| Flaw | Where it comes from | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voids and porosity | Gas trapped in the cast and the cure | Extra burning surface when the flame reaches them; a strain concentration near the bore |
| Cracks | Cool-down, temperature cycling, handling loads, ageing | Two new faces, open to the flame; can grow at ignition |
| Unbonds | A bond that did not form (a contaminated surface, a liner outside its cure window), or one that separated in a cool-down at a termination | Gas behind the grain; the web burns from both sides |
| Inclusions | Foreign material in the propellant | A local change of the burn rate and of the strength |
| Low-density zones | Poor flow or settling in the cast | A local change of the burn rate: the grain burns unevenly |

## The effect on the pressure

The pressure follows the burning area, $p \propto A_b^{1/(1-n)}$. A flaw that adds $\Delta A$ when the flame reaches it
raises the pressure by

$$
\frac{p'}{p} = \left(1 + \frac{\Delta A}{A_b}\right)^{1/(1-n)}
$$

### A void adds its own area

- **When the flame reaches a closed void,** the void fills with hot gas and burns outward, ahead of the main front. The
  front then carries a bump as high as the void is wide.
- **Huygens' construction gives the extra area of the bump exactly:** $\pi(R + h)^2$, where $R$ is the radius of the
  burning sphere and $h$ the height of its centre above the main front. $R + h$ stays equal to the void diameter $d$,
  so the extra area stays $\pi d^2$, the surface area of the void, until the bump reaches the case.
- **So voids add little.** Ten 5 mm voids add 785 mm², 0.16% of the burning area of the 300 mm grain at that point.
- **The bump reaches the case $d$ early,** so the insulation behind it sees the gas for a little longer.

### A crack adds its two faces

A crack open to the bore adds its two faces, $2cl$ for a depth $c$ and a length $l$. The faces lead the bore by $c$ until
the tip reaches the case. A shallow crack adds little. A crack through the web adds much more, and the gas reaches the
insulation behind it at ignition.

### An unbond at the end of the bond is the worst

- **An unbond in the middle of the cylinder is closed.** The gas cannot get into it until the web burns through, so it
  changes little.
- **An unbond at a termination is open to the port from ignition.** The gas burns the outer surface of the grain over
  its area, so that slice burns from both sides.
- **At ignition it adds its own area,** $2\pi b\,l$ for a band of length $l$ all round. At half the web the slice has
  gone, with its share of the bore surface, and the grain then has less area than before.
- **The insulation behind it sees the gas for the whole burn.** In the cylinder this is often the thinnest insulation in
  the motor, because the grain normally covers it until the tail-off
  ([Insulation](../../motor/insulation/#exposure-time-sets-the-thickness)).

### The shape of the trace decides

- **In a progressive grain** the early pressure is low. A rise at ignition does not set MEOP, but the exposure of the
  insulation still matters.
- **In a neutral or regressive grain** the same rise is on the peak, and it goes directly into MEOP.

## The effect on the structure

### The strain falls into the web

In a case-bonded tube, the hoop strain from the cool-down and from ignition is highest at the bore and falls into the
web:

$$
\varepsilon(r) = \varepsilon_\text{bore}\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^2
$$

One bore radius into the web, it is a quarter of the bore value
([Propellant grain](../../motor/propellant-grain/#part-2-structural-integrity)).

### A void concentrates the strain

- **A spherical void in an incompressible solid raises the strain around it.** From Goodier's elastic solution, the
  strain at its surface is $23/12 = 1.92$ times the remote strain in uniaxial tension, and $5/3 = 1.67$ times in shear.
  A void near a surface, a long void and a cluster concentrate more. Get the value for your flaw shapes from FEA.
- **A void is acceptable where its strain, with the factor, is within the capability.** Inside a depth $y_0$ below the
  bore it is not:

$$
y_0 = a\left(\sqrt{\frac{K\,\text{SF}\,\varepsilon_\text{bore}}{\varepsilon_\text{cap}}} - 1\right)
$$

- **Add one void diameter,** because a thin ligament between a void and the bore concentrates more.
- **A sealed void deep in the web is squeezed at ignition** by the pressure. In cold storage, with no pressure, it sees
  the cool-down strain.

### A crack at the bore grows

- **At ignition the gas fills the crack and the bore stretches.** The stress that opens the crack is the hoop stress net
  of the gas pressure on its faces. For an incompressible grain it is
  $\sigma = \tfrac43\,(E_\text{relaxed}\,\varepsilon_\text{cool-down} + E_\text{ignition}\,\varepsilon_\text{ignition})$.
- **A shallow crack of depth $c$ then has an energy release rate**

$$
G = \frac{1.12^2\,\pi\,\sigma^2\,c}{E'}
\qquad
E' = \tfrac43\,E_\text{ignition}
$$

- **It grows when $G$ reaches the fracture energy $G_c$** of the propellant at the rate and the temperature of the cold
  ignition.
- **The propellant is viscoelastic.** Its fracture energy and its modulus change with the rate and the temperature, and
  a crack can also grow slowly under a held strain (Schapery). Use this screen for its scale, and test your propellant.
- **The usual result is that no crack at the bore is acceptable.** The critical depth at a cold ignition is about a
  millimetre, smaller than radiography finds with confidence. Inspect the bore surface directly.

## Setting the acceptance criteria

- **Divide the grain into zones:** the bore surface and the band under it, the terminations and the flaps, the rest of
  the web, and the bondline in the cylinder. Each zone gets its own limits.
- **For each zone, find the critical flaw for each effect:** the pressure, the structure and the insulation. The
  smallest one governs.
- **Apply your programme's factor and write the limits:** the size of one flaw, the number of flaws in a volume, the
  spacing between them, and the area of an unbond.
- **Check that the inspection finds them.** A flaw that the criteria reject must be larger than the flaw that the
  inspection finds with 90% probability at 95% confidence ([NDT](../ndt/#probability-of-detection)). A limit that the
  inspection cannot find is not a limit.
- **Write what happens to a reject:** a repair that the design allows, an analysis of that motor, or scrap.
- **Inspect again after events that can open flaws:** temperature cycling, transport, drops, and ageing
  ([Storage & ageing](../../verify/storage-ageing/)).

## Worked example: the radiographs of the 300 mm grain

The grain is that of the [grain example](../../motor/propellant-grain/#worked-example-htpb-grain-in-the-d6ac-case): a
tube with a 100 mm bore and a 300 mm OD, 1000 mm long, with the propellant of the grain page. As a tube it is
progressive. A 56.8 mm throat makes it peak at the nominal 8.8 MPa of the
[MEOP build-up](../../motor/propellant/#worked-example-building-the-10-mpa-meop), and the burn takes 14.9 s. The
radiographs show three findings (illustrative): ten voids of 5 mm, 30 mm below the bore; a crack at the bore, 5 mm deep
and 100 mm long; and an unbond at the aft end, 50 mm long all round, open to the aft end face. The criteria and the test
values are **for this example only**: a 2% allowed pressure rise, 1 s of exposure for the cylinder insulation, the
illustrative capability of 39.6% from the grain page, a factor of 1.5, a modulus at the cold ignition of 40 MPa, and a
fracture energy of 1000 J/m². Push **Load worked example**.

1. **The voids, for the pressure:** $10 \times \pi \times 5^2 = 785$ mm² when the flame reaches them at 27.5 mm of web,
   0.16% of the area there. The pressure rises by 0.25%. Their bumps reach the case 5 mm early, so the insulation behind
   them sees the gas for 0.58 s.
2. **The voids, for the structure:** with $K = 2.0$, the cold-ignition bore strain of 16.1% and the factor, no void is
   acceptable within 5.2 mm of the bore. At 30 mm the strain is $16.1\% \times (50/80)^2 = 6.3\%$, and 12.6% at a void:
   MS = +1.10. **Accept.**
3. **The crack, for the pressure:** its faces add 1000 mm², 0.32% of the area at ignition. The pressure rises by 0.49%:
   small.
4. **The crack, for the structure:** the opening stress at the cold ignition is
   $\tfrac43(3.6 \times 0.104 + 40 \times 0.057) = 3.54$ MPa. The critical depth is 1.08 mm, and 0.48 mm with the factor
   on the stress. The 5 mm crack has MS = −0.69: it grows at the cold ignition. **Reject.** No crack at the bore that
   radiography can find is acceptable.
5. **The unbond:** the gas gets in at ignition. The extra area is $2\pi \times 150 \times 50 = 47\,100$ mm², 15% of the
   area at ignition, so the pressure at ignition goes from 1.62 to 2.01 MPa: +24% (2.02 MPa with the crack as well,
   as the calculator shows with all three findings). At half the web the slice has gone,
   and the final peak is 7.6% lower, at 8.13 MPa. MEOP is safe, but the insulation behind the unbond sees the gas for
   14.7 s, the whole burn, against the 1 s that it is sized for. **Reject,** unless the insulation there is made to last
   the whole burn.
6. **If the crack grows through the web,** 100 mm deep over the same length, its faces add 20,000 mm² at ignition: +10%.
   The gas also reaches the insulation behind it at ignition, for the whole burn.

<Aside type="tip" title="What the example shows">
  The flaw that looks worst on a radiograph is not always the one that matters. The voids are accepted. The small crack
  is a reject for the structure, not for its area. The unbond is a reject for the insulation, not for the pressure. Each
  zone and each effect gives its own limit.
</Aside>

## Where this stops

<Aside type="caution" title="Screening, not disposition">
  These relations give the scale of each effect and the shape of the criteria. The disposition of a real flaw needs the
  analysis of that motor, the test data of its propellant, and the authority of its programme.
</Aside>

- **The flame entering a crack.** A narrow crack may not let the flame in, but the gas still fills it and loads its tip.
  When a crack does burn, it can grow fast. This needs test data and specialist analysis.
- **Crack growth during the burn,** and the coupling between the growth and the pressure.
- **Slow crack growth in storage** under the held cool-down strain, and damage from temperature cycles.
- **Flaws near the grain ends, in flaps and at star points,** where the strain field is not that of a long tube: FEA.
- **Inclusions and low-density zones,** which make the grain burn unevenly, and move its centre of gravity off the axis
  ([Mass properties](../../motor/mass-properties/#a-bore-off-the-axis)).
- **The ignition transient.**

## On the drawing and in the specifications

- **The zones of the grain,** on the drawing.
- **For each zone:** the largest flaw, the number of flaws in a volume, their spacing, and the largest unbond.
- **The inspection:** the method, the coverage, the sensitivity, and the demonstrated probability of detection.
- **The disposition of rejects,** and who may approve a repair.
- **The inspections after events** (cycling, transport, drops) and in surveillance.
