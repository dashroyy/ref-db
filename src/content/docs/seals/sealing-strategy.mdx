## Why this matters

Each joint in a motor is a possible leak path for gas at MEOP and at approximately 3000 K. The joints are case to
closure, segment joints, nozzle to case, the igniter boss, and the pressure-transducer and test ports. A leak is
seldom a slow loss of pressure. Hot gas cuts a channel through metal very quickly. The Challenger accident started at a
booster field joint, where the O-rings could not follow the joint as it opened on a cold morning.

The O-ring page sizes one seal. This page is about the **strategy**: which seal, where, how many, how to check them, and
how the joint keeps them working.

## Seal types

| Seal | How it seals | Good for | Possible problems |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| O-ring, bore (radial) | Squeezed between bore and spigot; pressure pushes it onto the far groove wall | Closures, nozzle to case, igniters | Radial growth of the bore, extrusion gap, cold resilience |
| O-ring, face (axial) | Squeezed in a groove in a flange face | Flanged closures, port covers | Flange lift and rotation; depends on bolt preload |
| Back-up rings | Stiff rings adjacent to the O-ring that close the clearance | High pressure or larger clearances | Install on the low-pressure side, or on the two sides if the pressure can reverse |
| Spring-energised PTFE | A metal spring inside a PTFE or filled-polymer jacket pushes the lips onto the surfaces | Wide temperature range, aggressive fluids, dynamic duty, low friction | Needs a fine finish and a split or open groove to install; little elastic recovery after a gap opens |
| Metal seals: C, E, metal O-rings, crush gaskets | Elastic or plastic metal contact under a high seating load | High temperature, long storage, aggressive fluids | High bolt load, very fine finish, little springback, usually used one time only |
| Port seals: O-ring bosses, bonded washers | An O-ring under the shoulder of a fitting | Transducer and test ports, plugs | The plug is part of the seal count |
| Thermal barriers and fillers: putty, RTV, fibre rope | Fill the joint gap in front of the seal | Keep hot gas and particles away from the elastomer | Not pressure seals alone; can trap gas or make a channel for it |
| Thread sealants: tapered pipe threads, anaerobics | Fill the thread clearance | Low-pressure fittings | Do not use as the only seal in a critical pressure path |

**Bore or face?** A bore seal sits on a spigot, so a flange lift has no effect on it. But all the radial growth of the
bore has an effect on it. The bolts clamp a face seal, so radial growth has no effect on it. But it opens when the
flange lifts or turns. Each type is the better choice where the other type is weak.

| Seal | Tolerates | Sensitive to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Face (axial) | Radial movement, a diameter mismatch between the parts | Joint gapping, flatness of the faces, loss of bolt preload |
| Bore (radial) | Axial movement, joint gapping | Dilation of the bore, eccentricity, the extrusion gap |

If the joint can open a little under load, a bore seal continues to seal while a face seal unloads. This one fact
decides many motor layouts.

**Which family for which duty:**

| Family | Temperature | Reuse | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elastomer O-ring | Set by the compound | Usually replace at each assembly | The default: low cost, tolerant of the hardware |
| O-ring with back-up rings | Set by the compound | Replace | Higher pressure or a larger extrusion gap |
| Spring-energised PTFE | Wide, cryogenic to hot | Sometimes | Dynamic duty, aggressive fluids, low friction |
| Metal C-, E- or O-ring | High | No | Beyond the limits of elastomers, or for very long storage |
| Crush gasket, bonded washer | Moderate to high | No | Ports, plugs and fittings |

## Redundancy that is real

Critical motor joints have two seals in series, a primary and a secondary, so that one can fail without a leak. This
works only if the two do not fail together:

- **Prevent common causes.** Two identical O-rings in the same gland region have the same cold, the same gap opening
  and the same contamination. They are two copies of one seal.
- **Add diversity where possible,** for example a bore primary with a face secondary, or seals at different stiffness
  and temperature.
- **Verify the two seals.** A secondary seal that was never tested is not verified. Do a leak check on each seal, or on
  the space between them, after assembly.
- **Count all the items in the path.** Port plugs, transducer bosses and the threads of the igniter all need their own
  seal and back-up.

After Challenger, the redesigned field joint of the Shuttle booster added:
- a capture feature that limits joint rotation;
- a third O-ring;
- an insulation flap that the pressure closes against the joint;
- heaters to keep the seals warm.

Later, the nozzle joints also got a carbon-fibre rope thermal barrier in front of the O-rings.

## Keeping the gland closed

The best seal is a seal whose gap never moves. Under pressure:

- **The bore grows.** A thin-walled case grows radially by:

$$
\Delta r = \frac{p\,r^2}{E\,t}\left(1 - \frac{\nu}{2}\right)
$$

  The growth is inversely proportional to the wall thickness at the seal seat. Put bore seals in a thick, stiff joint
  ring, not in the membrane.
- **The flange lifts or turns.** Keep the bolted joint well below separation ([Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/)),
  make the flanges stiff, and put face seals inboard of the bolts.
- **The spigot can help.** If the mating part grows with the case, the gap stays closed.
- **Temperature has a large effect.** A cold elastomer recovers slowly and shrinks. Keep the seals in their qualified
  range, with heaters or insulation if necessary ([Transient thermal](../../motor/transient-thermal/)).
- **Keep hot gas away from the seal.** Insulation, joint fillers and thermal barriers stop gas and particles before
  they get to the O-ring. The O-ring then only has to hold pressure.

## Leak-check ports

A port drilled into the space between two seals lets you apply pressure and monitor the pressure decrease:

$$
Q = \frac{V\,\Delta p}{t} \quad\text{(in standard cm}^3\text{/s with } \Delta p \text{ in atmospheres)}
$$

- **Temperature changes look like leaks.** The pressure of the trapped gas changes by $p_{abs}\,\Delta T/T$. A change
  of one tenth of a degree can be larger than the resolution of the gauge. Make the temperature stable and record it, or
  use a helium mass-spectrometer test for smaller limits.
- **The direction is important.** Pressure between two seals pushes the primary seal from its back, onto the incorrect
  groove wall. At ignition, it must move across before it seals. Where possible, test in the operating direction. The
  Rogers Commission also found that a higher leak-check pressure on the Shuttle field joint could blow holes in its
  insulating putty.
- **Plug the port and verify the plug.** After the test, the port is part of the pressure boundary. It needs a sealed
  plug, its own torque and its own check.

## Worked example: the aft closure seal

The aft closure of the 300 mm case seals on a radial O-ring with a 5.33 mm section. It has a **minimum squeeze of
10.8%** at the cold condition after compression set ([O-ring design](../o-ring-design/#worked-example-a-nozzle-to-case-radial-seal)).
MEOP is 10 MPa, and the closure spigot grows by approximately 0.02 mm.

1. **Seat in the plain cylinder** (1.37 mm wall, from [Case sizing](../../motor/case-sizing/)). The radius of the case
   grows by 0.68 mm, which is its full hoop strain of 0.45%. The gland opens by 0.66 mm, but the ring is compressed by
   only 0.58 mm. Thus **the seal unloads and leaks**. Never put a bore seal in the membrane.
2. **Seat in a 10 mm joint ring.** The growth is 0.094 mm and the gland opens by 0.074 mm. The squeeze is 9.4%, which is
   less than a 10% minimum.
3. **The seat wall for 10%** is 15.0 mm. Thus the joint ring is a 15 mm forging. Some hundred grams make the seal work.
4. **Redundancy:** two rings with a port between them. With 2 cm³ between the seals, 700 kPa, a 0.1 kPa gauge and
   10 minutes, the smallest leak that you can see is $3.3\times10^{-6}$ std cm³/s. But a drift of 0.1 °C moves the
   pressure by 0.27 kPa, which is more than the gauge resolution. Thus temperature control decides the result. For a
   permitted leak of $1\times10^{-5}$ std cm³/s (an example), a 200 s hold is sufficient with a stable temperature.
5. **Diversity:** the bolted closure flange from [Bolted joints](../../joints/bolted-joints/) does not separate at MEOP
   (17.7 kN against 32.1 kN). Thus a **face seal** at that flange would stay closed. The same mechanism cannot open a
   bore primary and a face secondary.

## Where this stops

- **Thin-wall growth only.** At the joint ring, the junction with the dome or the next segment bends and turns. To get
  the real gap opening, you need a joint analysis or a finite-element model.
- **Static.** At ignition, the gap opens in milliseconds. If a cold ring can follow is a
  [viscoelastic](../../fundamentals/viscoelasticity/) question.
- **Simplified pressure-decay physics:** ideal gas and constant volume. Helium permeation through elastomers sets a
  limit on very sensitive helium tests.
- **No model of erosion and hot-gas flow** through a gap. Keep the gas away from the seals.

## On the drawing

- **Seals and back-up rings by specification:** size, compound, hardness and lot traceability.
- **Gland dimensions and surface finish,** and the lubricant.
- **Seal seat wall and joint stiffness:** the dimensions that the gap analysis used.
- **Leak-test procedure:** pressure, medium, direction, hold time, temperature record and acceptance.
- **Port plugs:** seal, torque and verification. Replace the seals each time that the joint is opened.
